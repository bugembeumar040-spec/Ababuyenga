## TITLE

```
Your Friends Aren't Bad. That's The Problem.
```

44 characters — inside the 70 that display in full on mobile, well inside the
100 hard limit. No hashtag in the title, deliberately: the first three hashtags
in the description already render as clickable links above the title, so a
hashtag up here spends title budget for nothing.

## DESCRIPTION

```
Your friends aren't the problem. That's exactly why nobody notices it happening.

A Sunday league team signed a professional. Twenty minutes in, nobody could reach
his passes — so he stopped making them. By half time he was playing at their speed.
Not because he got worse. Because nobody made him prove he was better.

#Shorts #IslamicReminder #IbnAlQayyim #Deen #SelfImprovement

Ibn al-Qayyim wrote in al-Fawāʾid about two kinds of gathering between friends.
The first, he wrote, is a meeting built on sociability and passing the time — one
that carries more harm than benefit, the least of which is the corruption of the
heart and the wasting of time.

It is usually compressed to a single line: beware of mixing with those whose
company wastes your time and corrupts your heart, lest all your affairs be ruined.

You don't rise to your talent. You sink to your room.

So the question isn't whether they're good people. It's who you become in that
room.

This one was prompted by a reminder from Ustadz Muhammad Nuzul Dzikri.

Who's the one person in your life who makes you faster? Say their name below.
```

## WHAT THE AUDIT CHANGED

**The title lead was swapped.** The pack led with "You Don't Rise to Your Talent.
You Sink to Your Room." and offered "Your Friends Aren't Bad. That's the Problem."
as alt 2. The audit inverts that. Contrarian Claim is one of the three most
consistently viral title patterns across niches in 2026 because it stacks
curiosity and self-relevance in one sentence — and alt 2 is a textbook one, while
the main title is a memorable line that means nothing to someone who hasn't
watched yet. "Your room" only pays off *inside* the video. The pack's own note
says lead with alt 2 if you want the comment section; the audit says you want the
comment section, because engagement is what the 2026 algorithm actually weighs.

**Hashtags moved out of the title and got cut to five.** Best practice is 3–5 in
the description. The hard rule worth knowing: go past 15 hashtags total and
YouTube ignores *all* of them on that video, not just the excess.

**The description got front-loaded.** The pack asked for 600+ characters with the
full citation, and that is preserved — but current practice is that short, precise
descriptions beat long unfocused ones, so the hook and the story now sit in the
first three lines where the algorithm and the viewer actually read, and the
citation sits below the fold where it can feed the comments.

**Nothing football went in the title.** The pack flags that a chunk of the
audience swipes on the word "league". The title avoids the sport entirely; only
the third alternate leads with it, and that one is there to be tested, not
assumed.

## FLAGS

**The cut runs 67s.** That is a legitimate Short — the limit has been three
minutes since October 2024 — but the best-performing window is 30–60s, so this
sits just outside it. Not worth recutting for. Worth knowing if it
underperforms, because length will be the first thing to suspect.

**This is a sincere religious reminder, and the title is as aggressive as it
should get.** A harder clickbait framing would almost certainly out-click it and
would sit badly against an Ibn al-Qayyim citation. If you want to push, test the
third alternate rather than sharpening the first.

**Accuracy holds.** The description says "wrote", not "said". No page number —
editions differ and a wrong one is the easiest correction in your comments to
make and the hardest to argue with. The "some of our teachers said" chain from
the source is not reproduced, because that framing belongs to the ustadz relaying
it, not to the printed text. "You don't rise to your talent" is left unquoted and
unattributed, because it is a rhetorical echo of a habit-literature line and not
anyone's citation.
