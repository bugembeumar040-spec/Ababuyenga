WHAT THIS FILE IS

One file, in cut order. Each block carries its final v3 narration and the stills
that sit under it, so you can work top to bottom without cross-referencing a
scene map. The VO here is the pacing-corrected version — paste each block into
v3 as its own generation.

STILLS ONLY, on purpose. The credit-card pack offered a video track because it
was 55 seconds. This is 5:47. Twenty-eight generated clips is four times the spend
and four times the drift, on a film whose entire register is stillness. Generate
stills, move them slowly in the edit. Six clips are worth shooting as video and
they are listed at the bottom under WORTH GENERATING AS VIDEO — motion that
carries meaning, nothing else.

TIMINGS ARE TARGETS, NOT THE CUT. Derived at 138wpm plus two seconds of dead air
per block, which is roughly what the retimed tags will produce. Record the VO
first, then re-derive the real IN/OUT points before you commit to holds.
Generating to estimated timings is how you end up trimming good frames.


================================================================================
THE ANICONIC RULE — READ THIS BEFORE ANYTHING ELSE
================================================================================

Three hard constraints. Every prompt below is already written to respect them.
If you rewrite a prompt, keep them.

  1. NO PROPHET IS DEPICTED. Block 6 is about Yaqub, peace be upon him, and
     there is no figure in it. Gates, footprints, hands, dust — the beat is
     carried entirely by what he did, never by him. Do not "just add a
     silhouette." A silhouette is a depiction. This is not a style preference
     and it is not negotiable in the edit.

  2. NO FACES ANYWHERE. The house blank-head build carries over from the Klarna
     and credit-card packs unchanged — smooth matte tan block, no eyes, no nose,
     no mouth. It was a style choice there. Here it is doing real work, and it
     is the reason this pack can show a fortune-teller and a client at all.

  3. NO GENERATED ARABIC. Ever. Image models produce Arabic-shaped gibberish,
     and on Quranic text that is not a quality problem, it is a different kind
     of problem entirely. Every Arabic frame below is a blank plate. You burn
     real typeset text in during the edit, the same rule the credit-card pack
     applies to numbers.

Palette carries over intact: deep navy #1E3A5F, terracotta #E85F42, cream
#E8DCC8, near-black surround, one warm amber accent, cool neutral grade. Keep
it. The channel reads as one thing when the palette doesn't move, and this
subject does not need a new colour language — it needs the one you already own.


================================================================================
TEXT & ARABIC — burn these in during the edit, never generate them
================================================================================

Every prompt that needs text asks for a blank plate on purpose.

  S04  AL-FALAQ 113:5  (Arabic, then the English under it)
  S06  al-'aynu haqq  ·  "The eye is true."
  S07  ح  س  د        (set one letter per block face, large)
  S12  the four refuges, set as four short English lines
  S13  إِذَا حَسَدَ  ·  "when he envies"
  S23  (a name-shaped blank — burn in nothing, that is the joke)
  S24  AL-FALAQ · AN-NAS  ·  11 VERSES  ·  NO FEE
  S28  the closing four lines

Set Arabic from a real typeset source — a clean mushaf render or a proper
Uthmani font — never from an image model, never from a screenshot you cannot
verify. Have a second pair of eyes read every Arabic frame before publish. A
transposed letter in a Quranic frame is the one error this audience will not
let go, and correcting it after publish means a reupload, not an edit.

S13 is your typographic hero. Give إِذَا حَسَدَ the most weight in the film —
it is the whole thesis in two words.

ACCURACY — the claims that need to survive a comment section
================================================================================

"THE QURAN MENTIONS THE ENVIER. ONCE." — this is the line that will draw the
top comment, and as written it is loose. The root ḥ-s-d occurs in several
places, among them 2:109, 4:54, 48:15 and 113:5. What is singular is the
refuge: Al-Falaq 113:5 is where the envier appears as a thing to seek refuge
FROM. Tighten the line to say that and the objection disappears:

    [measured] The Quran names the envier once as something to seek refuge
    from. Once... plainly... by the act itself.

Costs you four words and closes the only real hole in the script.

"FOUR THINGS NAMED. FOUR REFUGES." — correct. Al-Falaq seeks refuge from the
evil of what He created, of darkness when it settles, of the blowers on knots,
and of an envier when he envies. Four.

"ELEVEN VERSES BETWEEN THEM" — correct. Al-Falaq is five, An-Nas is six.

"AL-'AYNU HAQQ" — the hadith is in both Bukhari and Muslim. Solid ground. The
script attributes it plainly and claims nothing further, which is the right
posture.

AL-QALAM 68:51 and YUSUF 12:67 / 12:5 — all three are paraphrased in the VO
rather than quoted, which is correct for a script and avoids setting an English
rendering as though it were the text. Keep the paraphrase in the VO and put the
citation in the description.

"IT SPEAKS TO THE ENVIED FAR LESS THAN IT SPEAKS ABOUT THE ENVIER." — this is
framing, not a statistic. Never put a number on screen next to it, and do not
let it drift into "the Quran says X times" in the description. As a shape it is
defensible; as a count it is a fight you have no reason to pick.

NO RULING IS ISSUED ANYWHERE IN THIS SCRIPT, and that is deliberate. The video
describes what the Quran does and does not do; it never declares a verdict on
ruqyah practitioners as a class. Hold that line exactly as the credit-card pack
holds it on cards. The people who will argue in the comments are arguing about
a ruling you never made, and the script's best defence is that it genuinely
never made one.
