WHY THIS TOPIC, AND WHY NOW
================================================================================

UNCOVERED. Across all 15 uploads in the feed you have done mortgages, inflation,
fractional reserve, savings, investing, paper gold, BNPL, credit cards (three
times), pensions and student loans. Car finance is the largest consumer debt
product in Britain that you have never touched.

TIMELY, WITHOUT BEING A NEWS VIDEO. The FCA's motor finance redress scheme
opened on 30 June 2026 for agreements taken out from 1 April 2014, and opens on
31 August 2026 for earlier ones — roughly three weeks after this pack is dated.
Around 12.1 million agreements are in scope, average redress about £829, roughly
£7.5bn total. Search demand for "car finance" is going to spike into that date.

But the video is NOT about the redress scheme. The scheme goes in the
description, where it does the search work. The film does what your channel
does: one mechanism, followed all the way through. News dates the video. The
mechanism doesn't.

THE ARGUMENT — and it is not the obvious one.

The lazy take is "you don't own the car, so it's haram." That take is wrong, and
saying so out loud is this video's retention device. Renting is renting. Islam
has a name for a lease and it is ijara. Not owning the thing is fine.

The actual problem is narrower and much harder to shrug off:

  On a PCP you are charged interest on the balloon — the optional final payment,
  the lump you were always going to walk away from. It sits in the financed
  amount for the whole term. You pay a price for time on a slab of car you were
  never going to own.

So you carry a buyer's cost and a tenant's rights at the same time. That is the
two-flow structure your channel already does well — the same shape as the
student loan video's capped-payment / uncapped-interest split.

NO RE-DIAGNOSIS. Do not re-explain what riba is. Ten uploads have done that.
This film assumes the viewer knows and spends its 58 seconds on one mechanism
they have not seen.

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Three years of payments, and then you give it back. That is not the problem.

  On a PCP the finance company is the owner for the whole term. You are the
  registered keeper — the person the DVLA writes to — which is not the same
  thing and was never meant to be. So you are renting. Renting is renting, and
  a lease has a name in Islamic finance: ijara. Not owning the car is fine.

  Here is what isn't.

  A PCP splits the car into two parts. The part you pay off over the term, and
  a lump deferred to the very end — the optional final payment, the balloon,
  the Guaranteed Future Value. Most people never pay the balloon. Handing the
  car back is the normal exit; it is what the product is designed around.

  But the balloon sits inside the financed amount for the entire term, and
  interest is charged on the whole of it. Every month, for three years, part of
  your payment is a price for time on a slab of car you were always going to
  walk away from. That is why the monthly looks affordable and the total cost
  of credit doesn't.

  Two flows, not one. Money leaving you on a buyer's schedule. A logbook that
  never moves, on a tenant's terms. You carry the cost of ownership and hold
  the rights of someone renting.

  THE TWO-MINUTE CHECK:

  1. Find the optional final payment on your agreement. It is on page one, in
     its own box, and it is usually the largest single number on the document.
  2. Find the total amount payable and the total charge for credit. Subtract
     the cash price of the car. What is left is what time cost you.
  3. Decide honestly which one you are. If you intend to hand it back, you are
     renting — so compare it against a straight lease, where you are not
     financing a balloon you will never pay. If you intend to keep it, you are
     buying — so compare the total charge for credit against saving for it.

  Separately, and worth two minutes of its own: the FCA's motor finance redress
  scheme covers PCP and HP agreements taken out between 6 April 2007 and
  1 November 2024 where commission arrangements weren't properly disclosed. It
  opened on 30 June 2026 for agreements from 1 April 2014, and 31 August 2026
  for earlier ones. Around 12.1 million agreements are in scope. It is free to
  use and you do not need a claims company. Check the FCA's own site for where
  it currently stands — parts of it are subject to legal challenge.

  That scheme is about disclosure. This video is about the structure underneath
  it, which no redress scheme touches.

  This describes how these contracts are built. It is not a religious ruling —
  check with someone qualified before you act on it.

ACCURACY — the four claims that need to survive a comment section
================================================================================

"THE LOGBOOK WAS NEVER YOURS" — correct on both PCP and HP. The finance company
retains title until the final payment (and, on a PCP, the optional final payment
plus any purchase fee). The customer is the registered keeper, which the V5C
itself states is not proof of ownership. Do not say "you don't own the V5C" —
the distinction is keeper versus owner, and getting it precisely right is what
makes the rest of the video credible.

"INTEREST IS CHARGED ON THE BALLOON" — correct and central, so be exact about
why. The optional final payment is deferred, not excluded: it remains part of
the outstanding balance the whole term, so interest accrues on it throughout.
That is precisely why a PCP monthly is lower than an HP monthly on the same car
while the total charge for credit is not proportionally lower. If a commenter
says "but I never paid the balloon" — that is the point, and the video already
says it.

"IJARA" — a genuine lease contract in Islamic finance, and invoking it is the
honest move here because it concedes the strongest counter-argument up front.
But keep the concession narrow. The script says a lease is legitimate; it does
not say a PCP is an ijara. It isn't — an ijara's rental is a price for use, and
a PCP's finance charge is a price for time on a deferred principal. Do not let
the caption or the VO blur those, and do not let a comment reply do it either.

FCA REDRESS SCHEME — dated material, handle it accordingly. Confirmed 30 March
2026; opens 30 June 2026 for agreements from 1 April 2014 and 31 August 2026 for
earlier ones; covers 6 April 2007 to 1 November 2024; roughly 12.1 million
agreements, about £829 average redress, around £7.5bn total. As of 2 July 2026
the Upper Tribunal had suspended parts of the scheme by agreement with four
challengers. Because it can move, keep it out of the VO and out of the captions
entirely — it lives in the description, where you can edit it after publishing
without re-cutting the film. Point people at the FCA's own page rather than
restating the current status on screen, and never imply anyone is definitely
owed money.
