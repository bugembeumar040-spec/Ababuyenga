================================================================================
THE HONEST COST — read before you budget
================================================================================

The growth plan says the long-form is "the assets each pack already generates
and discards." That is true of the WORK. It is only partly true of the PIXELS,
and you should know the difference before you start.

WHAT TRANSFERS INTACT — no regeneration, no cost:

  · THE LOCKED CHARACTER REFERENCES. Reference images are aspect-agnostic. Your
    AMIR and shopkeeper frames drive 16:9 generations exactly as well as they
    drove 9:16 ones. This is the expensive thing and you already own it.
  · THE PROMPT LIBRARY. Every prompt in the three packs is reusable with one
    clause swapped. See the conversion rule below.
  · THE NEGATIVE PROMPTS. Unchanged, both tracks.
  · THE PALETTE, the caption spec, the burn-the-numbers rule, the no-faces rule.
  · THE VO PERFORMANCE DIRECTION. Same voice, same settings, same pacing logic.

WHAT DOES NOT TRANSFER — budget for it:

  · THE RENDERS THEMSELVES. A 9:16 master does not become a 16:9 shot. Every
    composition in the three packs puts its subject in the lower two-thirds with
    headroom above, which is precisely the wrong shape for widescreen. Cropping
    out produces a subject jammed at the bottom of frame with nothing at the
    sides. Pillarboxing the vertical masters inside a 16:9 frame reads as a
    reposted Short, which is the single clearest signal to a viewer that they
    are watching filler. Regenerate.
  · THE VO. Re-record. The long-form lines are new, and even the inherited ones
    are re-paced for a format where you are allowed to finish a thought.

So the real saving is roughly: you skip the design phase, the character
development, the style development and the script architecture, and you pay for
renders. On this pipeline that is most of the cost saved and the smaller part
paid. It is a good trade, but it is not free, and a plan that assumed free would
have run out of budget in chapter three.

SHOT ECONOMY. ~11 minutes at this contemplative pace is roughly 60 placements
drawn from a pool of about 45 unique assets. Long holds are correct for this
house style — 8 to 14 seconds on a still with a slow push reads as confidence,
not as padding, provided the VO underneath is dense. Returning to a hero shot
two or three times across the film is a legitimate device and cuts the pool
further. Do not try to generate a new asset for every beat; that way lies a
budget that never ships.

TRACK B REMAINS THE RECOMMENDATION. Stills, moved in the edit. At 11 minutes
the arithmetic that favoured stills on the Shorts becomes overwhelming, and
temporal artefacts that survive a 5-second clip become unwatchable across a
14-second hold.


================================================================================
THE 16:9 CONVERSION RULE — one clause, whole library
================================================================================

Every prompt in the three Short packs ends with a framing clause. Delete it and
paste the matching replacement. Nothing else in any prompt changes.

FIND (or any close variant):
  "9:16 vertical, subject in the lower two-thirds, clean headroom above"
  "9:16 vertical, card low-centre, dark space above"
  "Composed low in a 9:16 vertical frame with two-fifths of the frame empty above"

REPLACE — for CHARACTER shots (AMIR, the shopkeeper):
  "16:9 horizontal, 1920x1080. Subject standing on the left vertical third,
  facing into frame, full head clearance above. The right half of frame held as
  clean negative space falling into shadow, reserved for lower-third titles."

REPLACE — for MACRO and TABLETOP shots (cards, scale, folder, hourglass):
  "16:9 horizontal, 1920x1080. Subject centred on the lower-left third of frame
  with the surface running out to the right edge, wide lateral falloff into
  near-black, the upper right quadrant held empty for number cards."

REPLACE — for the FULL-SCREEN NUMBER beats (see TYPOGRAPHY):
  "16:9 horizontal, 1920x1080. Subject small and centred low in a deliberately
  empty frame, at most a fifth of the frame height, the remainder deep near-black
  with a single raking highlight. Composed for large type above the subject."

WHY THE ASYMMETRY. Widescreen has a horizontal axis your vertical compositions
never had to solve. Centring everything at 16:9 produces the flat, dead, stock-
explainer look this house style has so far completely avoided. Holding the
subject to one third and letting the other side go genuinely empty is what keeps
it looking composed rather than merely wide. It also gives the edit somewhere to
put type that is not on top of the subject, which at Shorts length you solved
with headroom and at long-form length you solve laterally.

ONE ADDITION TO EVERY 16:9 PROMPT. Append:
  "Wider environmental context visible at the frame edges, still falling into
  soft focus and shadow."
Vertical framing hid the world outside the subject. Widescreen shows it, and an
empty void at the sides of a 16:9 frame reads as a mistake rather than a choice.

-------- COLD OPEN (0:00–0:50) --------

[serious] The card industry has a word for people who pay their bill in full
every month.
[whispers] Deadbeats.
That's the actual term. Not an insult. A category.
And once you know that word exists, you cannot unsee what it tells you.
It tells you that paying on time makes you the wrong customer.
That the product was never the card.
And it tells you why, after searching properly, you will not find a single
Sharia-compliant credit card available anywhere in Britain.
[serious] This is how the thing actually works. All of it. In about eleven
minutes.

-------- CHAPTER 1 — WHO THE CARD IS ACTUALLY FOR (0:50–3:10) --------

Start with the vocabulary, because the industry's own words give the game away.
If you clear your balance every month, you are a transactor. If you carry one,
you are a revolver.
Those aren't slurs invented by critics. Revolver and transactor are the
regulator's own terms. The Financial Conduct Authority used them throughout its
credit card market study, which went through the account activity of thirty-four
million customers across five years.
[serious] So this isn't a theory about what banks might be doing. It is the
documented structure of the market, published by the body that supervises it.
Here is what that structure means.
A transactor borrows money for up to eight weeks and gives it back. The bank
earns almost nothing from them — a small cut of what the shop pays, and that
is it.
A revolver pays interest every month, indefinitely, and never quite reaches zero.
[emphatic] One of those two is the business.
Which explains the things that otherwise make no sense.
Your credit limit goes up without you asking. Nobody raises a limit for someone
who pays in full — there is nothing to gain. A higher limit only produces revenue
if some of it stays unpaid.
The cashback exists. One percent back, against a purchase rate in the low
twenties. That is not a gift. It is a discount on the bait.
And the minimum payment gets its own box on the statement, printed clear and
separate, the smallest number on the page. Not because it is the most useful
number. Because it is the most profitable one.
[serious] The regulator agrees, incidentally. It defines an account as being in
persistent debt when, over eighteen months, you have paid more in interest and
charges than you have paid off the actual balance. More to the bank than to the
debt. Since twenty-eighteen, firms have been required to spot those customers
and intervene.
Read that definition again. It had to be written because the outcome it describes
is common enough to need a rule.

-------- CHAPTER 2 — WHO PAYS WHEN YOU DON'T (3:10–5:05) --------

Now the part that catches people who think none of this touches them, because
they pay cash.
Every card payment costs the shop money. Not the shopper — the shop.
Be precise about this, because it is where most explanations go wrong. The fee
that goes to your bank, the interchange, is capped by law in this country. Zero
point three percent on a consumer credit card. Zero point two on debit. That cap
is not negotiable and it is not where the money is.
What the shop actually pays is a bundle: that capped interchange, plus scheme
fees to Visa or Mastercard, which are not capped and have climbed steadily since
the cap came in, plus the acquirer's margin on top. Together, typically somewhere
between one and a half and three percent.
[serious] Now compare that to buy-now-pay-later.
Because it isn't a card scheme, none of those caps touch it. Merchant rates for
instalment products commonly land near five or six percent. Multiple times what
a card costs the same shop on the same sale.
And no shop absorbs that. It goes into the price on the tag.
[emphatic] Which means the price went up for everyone. The person paying by card.
The person paying by phone. And the person who walked in, paid cash, took no
credit, signed nothing, and quietly covered part of the cost of everyone else's
instalment plan.
[calm] You do not have to use any of these products to be paying for them.

-------- CHAPTER 3 — THE QUESTION NOBODY ANSWERS CLEANLY (5:05–7:30) --------

Which brings us to the question this channel gets asked more than any other, and
the one you will not get a straight answer to anywhere — including here.
If I have a credit card, and I pay it in full every single month, and I am never
charged a penny of interest, is that permissible?
[serious] Understand first why the question is hard. It is not hard because
nobody has thought about it. It is hard because two serious positions exist and
both of them are reasoning from the same sources.
The first position says it is permissible, with conditions. The reasoning runs
like this. Riba is prohibited when it is taken or paid. A conditional clause that
is never triggered has not been paid and has not been taken. If you settle in
full within the interest-free window, no riba has occurred in fact. And in an
economy where a card is effectively required for a deposit, a hire car, a hotel,
or an online purchase with any protection attached to it, the need is real and is
weighed.
The second position says it is not permissible, and the reasoning is just as
direct. The contract itself stipulates interest on late payment. You have signed
an agreement to a riba transaction, and your intention not to trigger it does not
change what you agreed to. This position leans on the prohibition extending
beyond the two parties — to the one who writes the contract and the ones who
witness it. On that reading, the sin attaches to the agreement, not only to the
payment.
[serious] Both positions are held by qualified people. Both are argued from
Qur'an and Sunnah. They reach different conclusions.
So this video is not going to tell you which one is correct. That is not what
this channel is, and frankly a ruling from a finance channel on YouTube is worth
exactly nothing — take it to a scholar who knows your madhhab and your actual
circumstances.
[calm] What this video can tell you is the thing that is genuinely useful, and
that both positions agree on completely.
Neither of them is an argument for carrying a balance. Not one scholar on either
side of this holds that revolving credit card debt at twenty-four percent is
fine. The disagreement is entirely about the transactor — the person who never
pays interest at all. On the revolver, there is no disagreement to have.
[serious] So if you are carrying a balance right now, the difficult question
isn't yours yet. You have a simpler one in front of you first.

-------- CHAPTER 4 — WHAT ACTUALLY EXISTS IN BRITAIN (7:30–9:05) --------

Suppose you have decided to leave conventional cards behind. What replaces them.
Here is the answer nobody wants to give you, so I will. There is no
Sharia-compliant credit card available to a retail customer in this country.
Not one. Islamic banking in Britain is real and it is regulated — current
accounts, savings, home purchase plans — but the credit card is a gap on the
shelf.
Other markets did build them, mostly in Malaysia and the Gulf, using three
structures. One charges a fixed service fee instead of interest. The other two
route the same debt through a commodity sale.
[serious] And those two have a problem. The majority within several of the
classical schools rejected them, on the reasoning that they are sale contracts
arranged specifically to arrive at interest-bearing debt by a different road. A
mechanism to reach a prohibited outcome legally. The fee-based structure has
wider acceptance, though it is not uncontested either.
Which is a large part of why none of the three crossed into British retail
banking. The demand exists. The consensus to build on does not.
[calm] So the halal card that actually exists is a debit card, on a
Sharia-compliant current account. Your own money, no credit line, no contract
stipulating interest, no question to resolve.
That is the entire product. It is unglamorous, it is available today, and it
resolves the whole of chapter three by making the question not apply to you.

-------- CHAPTER 5 — WHAT YOU GIVE UP, AND HOW TO REPLACE IT (9:05–10:45) --------

But do not switch without knowing what you are handing back. Two things, and
both are real.
[emphatic] The first is Section seventy-five. Under the Consumer Credit Act, if
you buy something on a credit card costing more than a hundred pounds and not
more than thirty thousand, the card company is jointly liable with the retailer.
Shop goes under, holiday cancelled, kitchen never fitted — you can claim from the
bank. And the detail most people never hear: you only need to have put part of
the price on the card. Pay a hundred and fifty pound deposit on a five thousand
pound job, and the whole five thousand is covered.
Debit cards do not have this. What they have is chargeback, which is a scheme
run by Visa and Mastercard rather than a right written into law. No minimum, no
maximum, usually a hundred and twenty days to claim — and no statutory
obligation behind it. It is better than nothing. It is not the same thing.
The second is your credit file. A debit card builds nothing. Spend fifty thousand
pounds through it over ten years and a lender still sees no history. That matters
the day you apply for home finance, including Islamic home finance, which
underwrites you on the same credit reference data as everybody else.
[calm] Both are fixable, without a credit card.
Register on the electoral roll. It costs nothing and it is the single fastest
improvement available to most people.
Report your rent. Services exist that read the rent payment out of your bank feed
and file it with the credit reference agencies. Check which agencies each one
reports to free, because they differ — and know that they report missed payments
too. It is a credit file, not a credit boost.
Put a utility bill in your own name and pay it on time.
None of those require borrowing anything.

-------- CLOSE (10:45–11:10) --------

[warmly] So — the card was never the product. You were.
And the fix isn't a better card. It is being the customer they cannot make money
from, which turns out to be the same thing as spending money you already have.
[serious] The three short videos this came from are linked below, if you want any
single piece of it on its own.
[warmly] And if there is a part of this you want properly, at length — say so.
That is how the last one got made.

READ THAT NUMBER AGAIN — 22 seconds is too long to hold one frame, even in this
style. The list above is deliberately not a shot list. It is a BLOCK list: each
entry is one subject, held for that duration, and each block wants splitting into
two or three angles on that same subject in the edit.

That is how the 30 blocks become the ~60 placements and ~45 assets estimated
under THE HONEST COST. A block splits by generating the same prompt at a second
focal length or a 20-degree angle change — same object, same light, same seed
family — and cutting between them. That reads as coverage. Introducing a new
object every 8 seconds instead would read as a slideshow, and would triple the
generation budget for a worse film.

The rule: the film should feel like it contains few objects, examined closely.
Not many objects, glanced at. Split for pace, never for novelty.

ACCURACY — what has to survive eleven minutes of scrutiny
================================================================================

Longer videos get read more carefully than Shorts. Everything below is checkable
and a commenter will check it.

ONE CORRECTION TO INHERIT CAREFULLY — INTERCHANGE. The Klarna video's spine was
merchant fees, and the Deadbeat pack describes card interchange as "the exact
same argument one layer down." At Shorts length that shorthand was harmless. At
eleven minutes it is a trap, because the two numbers are not comparable and the
distinction is easy to check.

  · INTERCHANGE — the portion going to the card issuer — is capped in the UK at
    0.3% on consumer credit and 0.2% on consumer debit. Not negotiable.
  · WHAT THE SHOP ACTUALLY PAYS is the merchant service charge: that capped
    interchange, plus uncapped scheme fees to Visa and Mastercard, plus the
    acquirer's margin. Commonly 1.5–3% all in.
  · BNPL INSTALMENT PRODUCTS commonly sit near 5–6% for the merchant, because
    BNPL is not a card scheme and the interchange caps do not reach it.

The VO in chapter 2 is written to that distinction and says "the fee that goes
to your bank, the interchange, is capped" before giving the merchant total
separately. Do not let an edit collapse those two sentences into one. The
argument gets stronger with the precision, not weaker: the reason BNPL costs the
shop several times what a card does is precisely that one is capped and the
other isn't.

"REVOLVER AND TRANSACTOR ARE THE REGULATOR'S OWN TERMS" — correct. The FCA used
both throughout its Credit Card Market Study (MS14/6), whose final findings
published in July 2016 and drew on the account activity of 34 million customers
across five years. Cite it in the lower third and in the description. Note the
US-slang point from the Deadbeat pack still stands separately: "deadbeat" is
card-industry jargon, primarily American, and the VO says "the card industry has
a word", which remains accurate and is not attributed to the FCA.

"PERSISTENT DEBT" — the FCA defines an account as in persistent debt where, over
18 months, the customer has paid more in interest, fees and charges than they
have repaid of the principal. Rules requiring firms to identify and intervene
took effect 1 September 2018 (PS18/4). Stated as written, this is safe. Do not
paraphrase it as "most people" or attach a population figure to it — the rule's
existence is the point, not its prevalence.

"SECTION 75" — Consumer Credit Act 1974. Joint and several liability of the card
issuer with the retailer, for items costing over £100 and no more than £30,000,
on credit cards only. Part payment brings the full price within scope; the £150
deposit on a £5,000 job is the standard illustration and it is correct. Chargeback
is a Visa/Mastercard scheme rather than a statutory right, typically 120 days, no
minimum or maximum. The VO's "better than nothing, not the same thing" is the
honest characterisation.

THE FIQH CHAPTER — the one that needs your sourcing, not mine. The script lays
out two positions and their reasoning faithfully, and issues no ruling. That
structure is sound and it is the channel's established posture. But it currently
attributes both positions generically, to "qualified people", and before you
publish you should replace that with specific sourced references — a named fatwa
body or scholar for each side, cited in the description with a link.

Do that sourcing yourself, from scholars you would actually follow. Do not let
me or any other model supply the citations: a fabricated or misattributed fatwa
in this niche is the single worst error this channel could make, and it is
unrecoverable. The script is deliberately written so that it remains accurate if
you attribute it properly and remains honest if you cannot — in which case say
"positions exist on both sides" and leave the sourcing to the description.

THE CENTRAL SAFEGUARD, unchanged from all three packs: the video describes
economics and reports scholarly positions. It never rules. Chapter 3 makes that
refusal explicit and out loud, which is stronger than the Shorts' silence on it,
and it is the line that makes the rest of the channel defensible.

"NO SHARIA-COMPLIANT CREDIT CARD IN BRITAIN" — market availability, not
principle. Re-check Al Rayan, Gatehouse, Nomo and UBL the week you publish, same
as the halal card pack says. Eleven minutes of authority makes a stale fact more
embarrassing, not less.
