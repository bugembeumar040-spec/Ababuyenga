WHAT THIS FILE IS

The prompt packs solve production. Production is not your problem — the house
style is locked, the character builds are locked, the accuracy posture is
better than most newsrooms. This file is about the other half: what happens to
a video after it renders.

It exists because of one line you wrote yourself in the Deadbeat pack:

  "you have no series framing across 21 uploads and it is the cheapest
   retention you have available."

That is the correct diagnosis. This file is the fix, plus the larger thing that
sits behind it, which is that the channel is currently pointed at the harder
and worse-paying of the two available monetisation doors.

Nothing in here asks you to change the house style. The style is the asset.


================================================================================
THE HONEST MATH — READ THIS BEFORE THE REST
================================================================================

There is no mechanism, paid or otherwise, that produces two million real
subscribers on request. Purchased subscribers are worse than useless: they are
an explicit Terms of Service violation, they arrive with zero watch time, and
they drag your engagement ratios down in exactly the signal YouTube uses to
decide whether to show a video to anyone. Channels that buy them do not get
monetised faster. They get monetised never. Do not let anyone sell you this.

What is real is that YouTube has exactly two doors into the Partner Programme,
and they are not equally hard:

  DOOR A — SHORTS.     1,000 subscribers + 10,000,000 valid public Shorts
                       views in a rolling 90 days.
  DOOR B — LONG-FORM.  1,000 subscribers + 4,000 valid public watch hours in
                       a rolling 12 months.

Every upload you have is 9:16 and 30–60s. You are queued at Door A.

Now the part that matters. Watch hours accrued in the Shorts Feed do not count
toward the 4,000. The doors are sealed off from each other. A channel that
publishes only Shorts is not making slow progress toward Door B — it is making
no progress toward Door B at all.

Put the two thresholds in the same units. Assume an 8-minute long-form video
holding 40% average view duration, which is an ordinary, unremarkable result
for explainer content:

  DOOR A     10,000,000 views          in 90 days
  DOOR B        ~75,000 views          in 365 days

That is the whole argument. Roughly a hundred and thirty times less traffic,
over four times the window. You do not need to get better at anything to walk
through Door B. You need to be standing at it.

And then the second blade. Once through, the two doors do not pay the same:

  SHORTS RPM, finance, UK/US audience     ~$0.07 – $0.17 per 1,000 views
  LONG-FORM RPM, finance, UK/US audience     ~$10 – $25 per 1,000 views

Two orders of magnitude. Your niche — UK personal finance, credit, mortgages —
is one of the highest-CPM categories on the platform, and Shorts is the single
format that throws that advantage away. Finance Shorts pay roughly double
gaming Shorts, which sounds good until you notice that double a rounding error
is a rounding error.

The uncomfortable summary: hitting your dream figure on Shorts alone would
earn less than a mid-sized long-form finance channel with 30,000 subscribers.
Two million is the wrong target. It is a vanity number that the Shorts format
makes simultaneously harder to reach and less valuable on arrival.

The right target is 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 long-form watch hours, and
then a real business behind it. That is a twelve-month goal, not a fantasy,
and everything below is built to get you there.

THE SERIES ARCHITECTURE — fixing the 21-upload problem
================================================================================

You already built a series without naming one. Klarna closes on "Follow — the
fix is next." Deadbeat lands on that promise and closes on "the halal card
fix is next." That is a three-part arc with a spine.

Name it. Number it. Put the number in the title.

  THE DEBT SERIES
    Part 1  You've Already Paid Klarna              [published]
    Part 2  They Call You a Deadbeat                [in production]
    Part 3  There Is No Halal Credit Card in Britain
                                                   [pack written, produce it]
            (promised in Part 2 as "the halal card fix" — the product turned
             out not to exist, so Part 3 is the reversal. See the pack.)

Rules for every series from here:

  NUMBER IN THE TITLE. "Part 2" or "#2" visible before the click. It converts
  a browse impression into a sequence obligation.

  ONE PINNED PLAYLIST PER SERIES. Set it as the channel's featured playlist
  while the series is running. Playlist autoplay is the only mechanism on the
  platform that delivers consecutive watch time without the viewer choosing.

  EVERY PART ENDS ON THE NEXT PART'S PROMISE. You already do this instinctively.
  Make it non-negotiable.

  THE LAST PART ENDS ON THE LONG-FORM. See below. This is the whole hinge.

The three-part Short arc is not the product. It is the trailer for the product.


================================================================================
THE LONG-FORM PIVOT — the actual move
================================================================================

Here is the thing your own documentation already half-discovered. The Deadbeat
pack says of Track B stills:

  "frames reusable as thumbnails and as b-roll later"

You built for reuse without naming what the reuse was for. It is this.

A Deadbeat pack is 11 scenes, a full VO script, a caption table, a numbers
list and 11 rendered assets, for 55.6 seconds of finished video. Three packs
is 33 scenes and roughly three minutes of finished footage. An 8–10 minute
long-form video is those three packs, in order, with connective tissue — a
cold open, three chapter transitions, and a close — plus the material you were
already forced to cut for the 60-second slate rule.

You are not adding a new production line. You are stopping throwing away 80%
of what each pack generates.

THE PATTERN, per series:

  1. Ship the three Shorts on the existing cadence. They are the top of funnel
     and they are what the Shorts Feed is for.
  2. Cut the long-form from the same assets plus the offcuts. Target 8–12
     minutes. This is where the watch hours and the $10–25 RPM live.
  3. Each Short's pinned comment and end frame points at the long-form.
  4. The long-form's description points back at the playlist.

WHAT LONG-FORM LETS YOU SAY THAT 55 SECONDS CANNOT. This matters more than the
mechanics. The Deadbeat pack's ACCURACY section is three careful paragraphs of
qualification that the Short has no room to voice — the US/UK jargon split, the
"representative not universal" caveat on the APR figures, the deliberate refusal
to rule on cards where interest is never incurred. In a Short that nuance lives
in a text file and dies there. In a 10-minute video it is the content, and it is
exactly the content nobody else in this niche is making. Your caution is not a
constraint on the long-form. It is the long-form's entire differentiator.

FORMAT NOTE. Long-form is 16:9. The house style survives the transition intact
— same palette, same clay render, same faceless framing, same burn-numbers rule.
Regenerate at 1920x1080 rather than reframing the 9:16 masters; the compositions
in the packs are built for a vertical subject in the lower two-thirds and will
not crop out to widescreen cleanly. Budget for that. It is the one real cost of
the pivot.


================================================================================
PACKAGING — rules derived from your own channel data
================================================================================

THE DECLARATIVE RULE. "Is Klarna Halal?" lost to "You've Already Paid Klarna."
Question titles invite a yes/no the viewer answers in their own head, and then
they scroll. Declaratives assert something the viewer cannot verify without
clicking. Apply this to the back catalogue: any of the 21 with a question-mark
title is a free retitle experiment, and retitling costs nothing and does not
reset a video's standing.

Keep the "Is X Halal?" phrasing where it earns its place — in the description
and in spoken script, where it is doing search-intent work rather than
click work.

THE RIBA SERP RULE. Already established in the packs and correct: keep "riba"
out of titles, English search results are dominated by the Royal Institute of
British Architects. Description and voiceover only. Extend the same test to any
term before it goes in a title — search it in a logged-out window first and
look at what is already ranking.

THUMBNAIL DISCIPLINE. The Deadbeat spec — single object, isolated, one word in
terracotta, nothing else — is right, and it should become the template rather
than a per-video decision. One object, one word, house palette. Thumbnails that
are visibly siblings do compounding work on a channel page that individually
clever thumbnails do not.

DESCRIPTIONS. The pack already flags this: 600+ characters, do not ship the
197-char boilerplate. On the long-form this matters considerably more than it
does on a Short — it is a genuine ranking surface. Put the FCA "transactors vs
revolvers" corroboration in there. Citations in the description are cheap
credibility in a niche where the comment section will test you.

TAGS. Under 500 characters total. YouTube silently rejects the overflow rather
than warning you.

take it to their own. That is also, not incidentally, the difference between a
channel that can take sponsorship from an Islamic bank and one that cannot.

WHERE THE MONEY ACTUALLY IS
================================================================================

AdSense is the floor, not the ceiling, and in this niche it is not even the
main event. Ranked by realistic value per subscriber:

  1. AFFILIATE AND REFERRAL. UK Islamic finance has real products with real
     referral programmes — Islamic banks, halal investment platforms, Sharia
     screened funds. A finance audience of 20,000 engaged UK subscribers is
     worth considerably more here than the same audience is worth in AdSense.
     Disclose every one of them, on screen and in the description. Non-negotiable.

  2. SPONSORSHIP. Directly negotiated, priced on your audience's value rather
     than on raw view count. This is where your accuracy discipline converts
     into money: an Islamic bank's compliance team will clear a channel that
     refuses to issue rulings, and will not clear one that does.

  3. A PRODUCT. The obvious one already exists in outline — the halal mortgage
     and pension research you will do for Months 2 and 3 is a paid guide.

  4. ADSENSE. Long-form, £10–25 per 1,000. Real, but fourth.

This is why 2,000,000 is the wrong number to be chasing. Twenty thousand
subscribers who trust you on a £300,000 mortgage decision are worth more than
two million who watched a 40-second clip in a feed and scrolled on.


================================================================================
WHAT TO MEASURE
================================================================================

Weekly, in Studio. Four numbers, and only four:

  LONG-FORM WATCH HOURS, TRAILING 12 MONTHS.  The only number on the path to
    Door B. Everything else is diagnostic.
  AVERAGE VIEW DURATION AS A % ON LONG-FORM.  Below 30%, the problem is the
    first 30 seconds. Above 50%, make them longer.
  SUBSCRIBERS PER VIDEO.  Which topics convert a viewer into a subscriber.
    This is your slate-planning signal, not raw views.
  IMPRESSION CLICK-THROUGH.  Title and thumbnail only. Test retitles against it.

Ignore: total subscriber count, total views, and every third-party "estimated
earnings" figure. None of the three changes a decision you will make.

REVIEW POINT — DAY 90. If long-form average view duration is holding above 35%
and the trailing watch-hour figure is climbing, keep the cadence and do nothing
clever. If it is flat, the problem is the first thirty seconds of the long-form
cuts, not the topics — the topics above are correct.
