WHAT CHANGED AND WHY

The previous version of this file shot this Short in stylised low-poly 3D:
matte clay, near-photorealistic texture, deep near-black backgrounds, and
COMPLETELY BLANK featureless heads on every character. That pack is still in
git history at commit cebb512 if you want to compare.

This version reverts to the visual system used in the early uploads and in the
Deen & Money series bible: flat 2D vector, thick dark-brown outlines, warm cream
ground, muted editorial palette — and characters with faces.

The single most important change: FACES ARE BACK, AND FACELESSNESS IS NOW
RESERVED FOR THE INSTITUTION.

  The old bible already had a faceless device. EP01 shot 7 puts "one featureless
  grey silhouette figure" behind the bank counter. Facelessness meant the
  institution — the thing with no eyes to meet.

  The 3D pack made everyone blank: the cardholder, the clerk, all of them, with
  `eyes, mouth, nose, face, portrait` in the negative prompt on all 11 shots.
  That did two things. It removed the strongest retention and thumbnail asset
  short-form has. And it collapsed a contrast that was doing argumentative work
  — when only the bank is faceless that reads as a point being made; when
  everyone is faceless it reads as a limitation of the renderer.

Four supporting reasons the flat-vector system suits this channel better:

  BRIGHTNESS. Full-frame cream #F5EFDC is high-luminance. It holds up in a dark
  feed, at low phone brightness, and at thumbnail scale. Near-black with one
  hard key and shallow depth of field turns to mud at 2cm.

  CAPTION CONTRAST. This format burns numbers and captions over the image. Flat
  cream is a clean, predictable bed for type. Near-black with amber accents and
  bokeh is not, and it forces heavy outlines that read as amateur.

  DRIFT. Thick-outline flat vector is the most forgiving style there is for
  character consistency across many frames. Low-poly 3D plus photoreal texture
  plus blank heads is the highest-drift combination available — the 3D pack said
  so itself: "Character drift between clips is what kills this format."

  VELOCITY. At 21 uploads, cadence moves the needle more than per-frame polish.
  Flat stills are a fraction of the cost and time of 11 generated cinematic clips.

HONEST CAVEAT ON THE PERFORMANCE READ. The decision to revert came from channel
analytics that are not in this repo — it is taken on the channel owner's read of
the numbers, not independently verified here. Note also that the weakest recent
upload ("Minimum Payments Explained", 198 views) was also a topic problem:
already-covered ground, bottom-quartile subject. Style is very unlikely to be
the only variable. Revert the visual, but keep testing the topic slate too.

TRACK. One track only: STILLS, moved in the edit. The reverted style is a
flat-vector explainer idiom — it was authored as image prompts and animated in
post, and that is what performed. Do not text-to-video flat vector art; the
outlines boil and the faces drift. An optional image-to-video motion pass for
three specific shots is at the end of this file.

TIMINGS BELOW ARE TARGETS, NOT THE CUT. Record the VO first, then derive the
real IN/OUT points into video/src/creditcard/beats.ts before generating.
If the recorded VO runs past 60s, cut from scenes 06–08 (the list section).
Never cut from 10–13.

PACING NOTES

  "Deadbeat" is the whole video. Cold open — no run-up, no throat-clear, the
  word is the first thing in the file. Give it a full beat of silence after it
  and do not let the next line ride in on it. If v3 rushes it, generate scene

SCRIPT AUDIT — what changed from the previous draft, and why
================================================================================

Requested before release. Five findings, all applied above.

1. THE HOOK WAS BURYING ITS OWN PAYLOAD — fixed, highest impact.

   Previous: "The card industry has a word for people who pay in full every
   month. / Deadbeats."

   That is thirteen words of setup — roughly four seconds — before the

5. ONE WORD WAS SLIGHTLY WRONG ON THE FACTS — changed.

ACCURACY — the three claims that need to survive a comment section
================================================================================

"DEADBEAT" — real industry jargon for a cardholder who pays in full and
generates no interest revenue, contrasted with "revolver". It is primarily US
card-industry slang. The VO says "the card industry calls you", which is
accurate and does not claim it is a UK term. Keep that phrasing. UK regulatory
language for the same split is "transactors" vs "revolvers" — that is the FCA's
own vocabulary in its credit card market work, and it belongs in the
description as corroboration.

"1% BACK vs 24% APR" — representative, not universal. UK cashback on mass
market cards typically sits well under 1%, and representative purchase APRs on
mainstream UK cards commonly land in the low-to-mid twenties. Both figures are
in range and the caption presents them as an illustration, not as one specific
product. Do not name an issuer on screen.

"Riba isn't a fee you might trip over. It's the revenue model." — this is a
claim about the product's economics, not a fatwa, and the script deliberately
never issues a ruling in your own voice. That is the same posture as the Klarna
pack, which let murabaha carry the contrast rather than declaring a verdict.
Hold that line — the fiqh on cards where interest is never actually incurred is
genuinely differed, and a verdict in the VO invites the one argument you cannot
win in a comment reply.
