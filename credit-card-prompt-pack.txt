================================================================================
WHY THE VISUAL WAS REVERTED
================================================================================

The previous version of this pack shot the Short in stylised low-poly 3D: matte
clay, near-photorealistic texture, deep near-black backgrounds, and COMPLETELY
BLANK featureless heads on every character. That pack is in git history at
commit cebb512.

This one reverts to the system used in the early uploads and in the Deen & Money
series bible: flat 2D vector, thick dark-brown outlines, warm cream ground, muted
editorial palette — and characters with faces.

THE CENTRAL CHANGE: FACES ARE BACK, AND FACELESSNESS BELONGS TO THE INSTITUTION.

  The bible already had a faceless device. EP01 shot 7 puts "one featureless grey
  silhouette figure" behind the bank counter. Facelessness meant the institution
  — the thing with no eyes to meet.

  The 3D pack made everyone blank: the cardholder, the clerk, all of them, with
  `eyes, mouth, nose, face, portrait` in the negative prompt on all 11 shots.
  That removed the strongest retention and thumbnail asset short-form has, and it
  collapsed a contrast that was doing argumentative work. When only the bank is
  faceless, that reads as a point being made. When everyone is faceless, it reads
  as a limitation of the renderer.

Four supporting reasons the flat-vector system suits this channel better:

  BRIGHTNESS. Full-frame cream #F5EFDC is high-luminance. It holds up in a dark
  feed, at low phone brightness, and at thumbnail scale. Near-black with one hard
  key and shallow depth of field turns to mud at 2cm.

  CAPTION CONTRAST. This format burns numbers over the image. Flat cream is a
  clean, predictable bed for type. Near-black with amber accents and bokeh is
  not, and it forces heavy outlines that read as amateur.

  DRIFT. Thick-outline flat vector is the most forgiving style for character
  consistency across many frames. Low-poly 3D plus photoreal texture plus blank
  heads is the highest-drift combination available — the 3D pack said so itself.

  VELOCITY. At 21 uploads, cadence moves the needle more than per-frame polish.

HONEST CAVEAT. The decision came from channel analytics not in this repo — it is
taken on the channel owner's read, not independently verified here. The weakest
recent upload ("Minimum Payments Explained", 198 views) was also a topic problem.
Style is unlikely to be the only variable. Revert the visual, keep testing the
slate.

TRIM RULE — APPLY BEFORE EDITING.

  Cap every silence at 0.5s, EXCEPT keep ~1.0s after "for paying in full" and
  ~1.0s after "Be worthless."

  Recovers 12.4s → lands at ~57.9s, inside the 30–60s slate rule. It also fixes
  pacing: the raw file has fourteen gaps over a full second, which reads as drag.

  CAUSE OF THE OVERRUN, for next time. The script was written with a blank line
  between every beat for pacing. v3 reads blank lines as paragraph breaks and
  adds roughly a second at each — 18 breaks pushed the air from 30% to 41.5%.
  Keep the blank lines for delivery, but budget ~1s per break when estimating
  runtime, or join beats into paragraphs and let punctuation do the pacing.

MIX NOTE. "For paying in full" is the most important line in the video and the
quietest. Normalise that segment up so it survives a phone speaker in a noisy
room.

HOOK — what changed and why it matters
================================================================================

  RECORDED (v1, old script)   self-ID 2.8s · payload 4.2s
  FIRST REWRITE               self-ID 4.5s · payload 0.3s   ← wrong variable
  SHIPPING                    self-ID 1.6s · payload 1.9s

The first rewrite moved the payload word to 0.3s but pushed self-identification
out to 4.5s — later than the original. Self-ID is what stops the scroll, not
vocabulary. Cold-opening on "deadbeat" also fails because its everyday meaning
(someone who doesn't pay) is the opposite of the video's meaning (someone who
does, in full) — so a cold open resolves against the interesting reading.

"The card industry calls you a deadbeat" fires self-ID and attribution in one
move: about the viewer, and clearly not the creator's judgment of them. The
whisper sits on "for paying in full" because the surprise is the reason, not the
word.

OTHER AUDIT FINDINGS APPLIED
  · "Revolver" cut — 4.9s spent on a synonym that never paid off later.
  · Middle no longer runs three identical "which is why…" clauses; the thesis
    line moved onto a bare face at the 37s mark, where a 60s Short sheds.
  · CTA no longer repeats Klarna's "Follow — the fix is next" verbatim.
  · "Twenty-four point nine percent owed" → "twenty-four percent charged." APR
    is charged on a carried balance, not flatly owed.

LEFT ALONE ON PURPOSE. "Be worthless to them" is the best line in the script.
The riba framing still makes a claim about economics and never issues a ruling
in your own voice.

Derived from the EP01 series bible's own scoreboard card — cream panel, thin
charcoal border, heading strip, hairline rows, label-left / value-right, heavy
rule under the total. Nothing here is imported from another channel.

TYPE

              tracking -1%.
  CAP2        Inter Medium. Sentence case. 46px, 80% opacity.
  ACCENT      Anton 150px. Used on `A DEADBEAT` and `BE WORTHLESS` only.

COLOUR
  On cream    CAP1 and CAP2 charcoal #3E2B23, accent word brick red #B34A32.
  On GEN 2    everything cream #F5EFDC — the grey frame kills charcoal type.

POSITION
  CAP1 top at y=270px (14%). CAP2 baseline at y=440px (23%). Safe margins 90px.
  EXCEPTIONS: cuts 03 and 13 go in the BOTTOM third until those images are
  repositioned. Cut 06's CAP2 moves to y=21%.

ANCHORING — the rule that was missing
  A burn-in tied to a feature IN the image (£25 in its printed box, a card
  sitting on a table) must ride the camera move, or it drifts off its mark as
  the push runs. Those cues are marked ANCHORED in the cue sheet and are
  transformed by the live zoom each frame:

      screen = (design - centre) * z + centre - offset

  Captions that sit on the frame rather than on the picture — every CAP1 and
  CAP2 — stay locked to the frame and do NOT anchor.

  This is why £25 was outside its box in the first render: the 11% push moved
  the box roughly 56px down over the hold while the number stayed put.

MOTION
  CAP1        cuts on hard. No fade, no movement — the house style is locked-off
              and the type should match.
  CAP2        fades up over 5 frames with a 10px rise.
  NUMBERS     cut on hard. They are evidence, not decoration.
  ALL         cut out hard, 200ms before the image cut.
  NEVER       slides, bounces, typewriters, drop shadows, glows.

THREE CARDS

  CARD A — LINE ITEM. Cream panel, 2px charcoal border, square corners. Label
  left (32px, letterspaced +8%), value right (72px). One hairline beneath.
  For single figures.

  CARD B — COLLISION. Same panel, split by a vertical hairline. Left cell small
  label + small figure. Right cell small label + figure at 190px in brick red.
  THE SIZE ASYMMETRY IS THE ARGUMENT — never balance the two cells.

  CARD C — STAMP. No panel. Figure at 78px charcoal, with a heavy charcoal rule
  directly beneath running 60% of frame width. For verdicts.

ACCURACY — the three claims that need to survive a comment section
================================================================================

"DEADBEAT" — real industry jargon for a cardholder who pays in full and generates
no interest revenue, contrasted with "revolver". Primarily US card-industry
slang. The VO says "the card industry calls you", which is accurate and does not
claim it is a UK term. Keep that phrasing. UK regulatory language for the same
split is "transactors" vs "revolvers" — the FCA's own vocabulary in its credit
card market work, and it belongs in the description as corroboration.

"1% BACK vs 24% APR" — representative, not universal. UK cashback on mass market
cards typically sits well under 1%, and representative purchase APRs on
mainstream UK cards commonly land in the low-to-mid twenties. Both are in range
and the card presents them as illustration, not as one specific product. Do not
name an issuer on screen. Screen and VO must agree — both say 24%.

"Riba isn't a fee you might trigger. It's the model." — a claim about the
product's economics, not a fatwa. The script deliberately never issues a ruling
in your own voice. Same posture as the Klarna pack, which let murabaha carry the
contrast rather than declaring a verdict. Hold that line — the fiqh on cards
where interest is never actually incurred is genuinely differed, and a verdict in
the VO invites the one argument you cannot win in a comment reply.
