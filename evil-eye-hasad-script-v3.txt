EVIL EYE / HASAD — ElevenLabs v3 narration script
Pacing pass: tags retimed for slower, more dynamic delivery.

Recommended voice settings:
  Stability: Natural (Creative if tags feel under-read)
  Speed: 0.90 - 0.95
  Render each BLOCK as its own generation; keep the block breaks.

================================================================
BLOCK 1 — COLD OPEN
================================================================

[somber] Somebody told you it was a look.

[slowly] A cousin at a wedding. An aunt who leaned over the pram... and said what a beautiful baby.

[deliberate] And then everything went wrong at once.

[whispers] And then somebody offered — for a price — to tell you which one of them it was.

[measured] The Quran mentions the envier. Once... plainly... by the act itself.

[long pause] And it never once tells you to find him.

================================================================
BLOCK 2 — THE FRAME
================================================================

[firmly] This is not a video that tells you the evil eye is not real.

[emphatic] It is real. The Prophet ﷺ said so... and the words are as short as words get.

[slowly] Al-'aynu haqq. The eye is true.

[thoughtful] So we are not arguing about whether.

[curious] We are going to look at what the Quran does with it — and, more usefully... at what it refuses to do.

================================================================
BLOCK 3 — THE WORD
================================================================

[measured] The word is hasad. Three letters... ha... seen... dal.

[deliberate] And it does not mean wanting what someone has.

[thoughtful] Wanting the like of it is a different word — ghibta — and it is not blamed.

[firmly] Hasad is wanting it GONE from them.

[slowly] Not up for you. Down for them.

[deliberate] That is the whole distinction... and almost nobody makes it.

[thoughtful] Which means hasad is a condition of a heart.
Not a beam.
Not a transaction.
A state someone is in.

================================================================
BLOCK 4 — THE LOOK THAT LANDS
================================================================

[measured] The Quran does describe a look that lands.

[slowly] In Al-Qalam, of those who disbelieved — that they would almost make you slip with their eyes.

[quietly] So here is where the Quran hands you something.

[deliberate] And notice what shape it is.

================================================================
BLOCK 5 — AL-FALAQ
================================================================

[serious] Four things named. Four refuges.

[slowly] And the last of them... wa min sharri hasidin idha hasad. From the evil of an envier, when he envies.

[deliberate] WHEN he envies. Idha hasad.

[measured] The Quran does not say "from the envier." It says from the evil of an envier at the moment he is envying.

[long pause] It is naming a condition. Not a person.

[firmly] You cannot build a suspect list out of a condition. And the Quran does not hand you one.

[warmly] There is a man in the Quran who feared this exact thing for his sons... and what he did about it is the most useful passage on the subject in the whole Book.

================================================================
BLOCK 6 — YAQUB
================================================================

[gently] Yaqub — peace be upon him — tells his sons not to enter by a single gate... but to enter by separate ones.

[measured] So he took a precaution. Real. Practical. Physical.

He did not pretend the danger was nothing.

[warmly] And then — in the same breath, in the same verse — he says the decision belongs to Allah alone... and that upon Him he relies.

[slowly] He takes the precaution...

[deliberate] He hands the outcome straight back.

================================================================
BLOCK 7 — THE NAME THAT ISN'T THERE
================================================================

[quietly] Read the passage again... and look for the part where he names who he is afraid of.

[whispers] It is not there.

[softly] No neighbour. No cousin. No aunt at the pram. Not one name.

[measured] Earlier in the same surah he had told Yusuf not to relate his dream to his brothers. Concealment. Not confrontation.

[deliberate] That is the Quran's posture in one line.
Cover the blessing.
Take the precaution.
Ask Allah.
Accuse no one.

[serious] Now hold that against what happens when somebody tells you they can see who did it.

================================================================
BLOCK 8 — THE PRODUCT
================================================================

[measured] If you watched the last video on this channel, you already know the shape of this. A man who says he can perceive what you cannot — and then names a price.

[deliberate] The Quran took that apart for the jinn. It takes it apart here the same way... and for the same reason.

[firmly] Because the naming is the product.

[slowly] Not the protection — the protection is free, and it is already in your hands.

[emphatic] The NAME is what costs money.

[softly] Al-Falaq and An-Nas. Eleven verses between them. No intermediary. No appointment. No fee.

================================================================
BLOCK 9 — THE TURN
================================================================

[thoughtful] And there is one more thing... and it is the part nobody expects.

[measured] Go through the Quran counting. It speaks to the envied far less than it speaks about the envier.

[slowly] The question it keeps putting is not "who did this to you."

[deliberate] It is — do THEY envy people for what Allah gave them?

[quietly] Which means the verse you were reaching for as a shield has a second edge... and it is pointed the other way.

================================================================
BLOCK 10 — CLOSE
================================================================

[measured] The eye is real. The envier is real.

[slowly] And the Quran left the name out on purpose... because the moment you have a name, you have somewhere to put this that is not your own hands.

[gently] Say the surah. Cover what you love. Ask Him.

[softly] And if the question "who did this" will not leave you alone —

[whispers] that question is worth asking about yourself first.
