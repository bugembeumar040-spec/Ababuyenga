WHAT THIS FILE IS

The shooting document for the jinn video. Same two-track architecture as the
credit-card pack: one complete way to shoot it with generated video, one with
generated stills moved in the edit. Pick one — the idioms don't cut together.

  TRACK A — VIDEO.  26 generated clips. Highest cost, highest drift risk.
  TRACK B — STILLS. 26 generated images, moved in the edit. RECOMMENDED HERE.

TRACK B IS THE RIGHT CALL FOR THIS ONE, and that's a change from the Short. A
55-second Short can carry 11 generated clips. A ten-minute long-form needs
roughly two and a half times the coverage, and generated video drifts across
that many shots in ways stills simply don't. Stills also give you thumbnail
frames and a reusable plate library for every future video in this series.
Track A is written out in full if you want it, but shoot B.

TIMINGS BELOW ARE DERIVED, NOT ESTIMATED. The channel's real narration pace was
measured off the de-duplicated Anger transcript: 851 spoken words across 377
seconds = 135 wpm. Every IN/OUT below is built on 135 wpm, not on a generic
150. Record the VO first and re-derive anyway before you generate anything on
Track A — but these are close.

SCRIPT AUDIT — WHAT CHANGED FROM THE DRAFT IN THE BRIEF, AND WHY
================================================================================

The draft script in the topic brief was structurally sound but had four
retention faults. All four are fixed in the VO below. Recording the brief's
version instead would cost you most of the benefit.

  FAULT 1 — THE TITLE PROMISE ARRIVED TWO MINUTES LATE.
  The title says "Iblis Was Never an Angel." In the draft, Iblis showed up at
  beat 03, around 2:05, behind a full minute of etymology. Everyone who clicked
  for the Iblis claim had to sit through a linguistics lesson to reach it. That
  is the single most expensive mistake available in the first minute.
  FIX: cold open on the Iblis contradiction. Verse on screen inside 25 seconds.
  The etymology then becomes the payoff for staying, not the toll for entering.

  FAULT 2 — THE PEAK WAS FOLLOWED BY AN ANTICLIMAX.
  Draft order was: death of Sulayman (the strongest beat in the video) → then a
  descriptive beat about what jinn built for him. Energy fell off a cliff
  immediately after the best moment.
  FIX: the capability material now runs BEFORE the death, as setup. You watch
  them build, dive and lift a throne, and THEN learn they couldn't see a dead
  man in the room. The peak now lands last and flows straight into the next
  correction.

  FAULT 3 — THE PINNED COMMENT ASKED A QUESTION THE SCRIPT COULDN'T SUPPORT.
  The pinned comment asks "which of these four did you believe?" — but the draft
  never numbered them out loud. Nothing for a viewer to map an answer onto.
  FIX: all four are now explicitly numbered in VO and on screen. This also
  builds a retention ladder — a viewer who has collected one, two and three
  will stay for four. That device is most of why list content retains.

  FAULT 4 — THE SUBSCRIBE ASK WAS IN THE WORST POSSIBLE PLACE.
  The audit found this channel asks at the very end, after the payoff, and
  converts at 1.7%. The draft repeated that mistake.
  FIX: the ask now sits at 7:05, in the two seconds after the Sulayman reveal —
  the emotional peak of the video. Nothing at the end but the close.

  ALSO: pace corrected. 135 wpm measured off the de-duplicated Anger transcript
  (851 words / 377s), not 150 assumed. The brief's "~9:30" was really about
  10:30. Script rebuilt to a counted 1,361 words, landing 10:05.

VISUAL SYSTEM
================================================================================

THE ONE IDEA. The root ج-ن-ن means "to conceal." So the entire film is shot
through things that conceal while letting light through. The mashrabiya — the
carved wooden lattice screen of Islamic architecture, whose literal function is
to hide what is behind it while passing light — is the spine motif. It appears
in seven of the eleven beats. It is the root, made photographable.

HARD RULES — these are not style preferences.

  NO FIGURES. No people, no faces, no hands, no silhouettes of persons. The
  channel is faceless and this topic makes that a doctrinal asset, not just a
  production shortcut.

  SULAYMAN IS NEVER DEPICTED. Not as a person, not as a silhouette, not as a
  shape on a throne, not implied by a shadow. Prophets are not depicted. Beat 07
  and 08 are carried entirely by objects, architecture and empty space. The
  staff in beat 08 is shown alone, standing, with nothing holding it.

  JINN ARE NEVER DEPICTED. No creatures, no entities, no eyes, no smoke-with-a-
  shape. The moment you draw one you have made a monster video and you will get
  the monster audience, which does not subscribe to tafsir channels. Jinn are
  conveyed as ABSENCE and as heat-distortion — the air bending, and nothing
  there.

  NO HORROR GRAMMAR. No red, no green glow, no fog machine, no crawling shadow,
  no candle-in-a-corridor. This is the whole differentiator against the content
  already ranking for this keyword.

  ARABIC IS NEVER GENERATED. Every image model garbles Arabic script, and
  garbled Quranic text in a Quran video is the one error that will end the
  channel's credibility in a single comment thread. All Arabic is burned in
  during the edit from real text. Every prompt below says "no text."

PALETTE
  near-black         #0A0C10   ground, 70% of every frame
  ember              #E8873C   the fire the jinn are made of — beats 03, 08 only
  parchment          #E8DCC8   text, paper, stone highlights
  muted gold         #C9A227   brass, lamplight, lattice edges
  deep garden green  #123B36   beat 02 only, on the janna word — nowhere else

  The ember/green discipline matters. Ember appears in exactly two beats so it
  reads as "the material they're made of," and green appears in exactly one so
  it reads as "the garden." Spray either everywhere and both stop meaning
  anything.

LIGHT. One hard key, always raking, always low. Long real shadows. Deep falloff
to near-black at the frame edges. The look is a single shaft of light in a dark
room — which is also, conveniently, exactly what a mashrabiya does to sunlight.

MOTION. Slow. Nothing in this film moves fast. No whip pans, no snap zooms, no
shake. Pushes are 5–10% total travel across a whole beat, eased out.

Long-form carries far less on-screen text than a Short. Three kinds only:

  A) THE VERSE CARD. On every citation. Arabic line, transliteration, English,
     reference. Held 4–6s, parchment on near-black, centred, no motion.
  B) THE ROOT CARD. Beat 02 only. The four cognates, revealed one at a time.

------------------------------------------------------------ BEAT 01 — COLD OPEN

[serious] Everyone knows the story. Allah commanded the angels to bow to Adam.
They all bowed. One refused. And that one was Iblis.

So Iblis was a fallen angel.

[pause] Except the Quran never says that.

Surah Al-Kahf, verse fifty. Same event. Same refusal. And then five words that
almost nobody notices.

Kana min al-jinn.

He was of the jinn.

Not an angel who fell. Something else entirely — and once you see it, three more
things you were probably told about jinn stop holding up too. The fourth one
should change what you actually do.

Start with the word itself, because the word gives the whole thing away.

------------------------------------------------------------ BEAT 02 — THE ROOT

"Jinn" is not a name.

It's a description. The root is jeem, noon, noon. And it means: to conceal. To
cover over.

You already know this root, even if you've never noticed it.

Janna — the garden, paradise. Same root. Because a garden is a place so thick
with growth that the ground beneath it is hidden.

Janeen — a fetus. Same root. Because the child is concealed inside the womb.

Majnoon — which gets translated "madman." Same root. Someone whose reason has
been covered over.

And junna — a shield. Same root. The thing you hide behind. [pause] Hold onto
that one. We come back to it at the end.

So when the Quran says jinn, it isn't saying demon. It's saying: the concealed
ones. The ones you cannot see.

That's the whole category. Not evil. Not monstrous. Hidden.

--------------------------------------------------------- BEAT 03 — MADE OF WHAT

The Quran is specific about what they're made of.

Surah Ar-Rahman, verse fifteen. He created man from clay like pottery — and He
created the jinn from a smokeless flame of fire. Marij min nar.

And Surah Al-Hijr, verse twenty-seven: and the jinn We created before, from
scorching fire.

Two things there. Before — jinn were created before human beings. And marij:
not a log burning. Not smoke. The pure moving part of a flame. The part with no
substance you could hold.

Which fits the root exactly. Made of the thing you can see straight through.

------------------------------------------------- BEAT 04 — MISCONCEPTION ONE

So. Number one. Iblis was never an angel.

[serious] And that isn't a technicality. It's the entire moral logic of the
story.

Angels, as the Quran describes them, do not disobey. That is what they are. They
do what they are commanded.

Iblis could refuse — because Iblis had the one thing angels don't. A will. The
same thing you have.

He wasn't a perfect being who malfunctioned. He was a being who could choose,
and chose wrong.

Which is why his story is aimed at you. It only works as a warning if he was
never an angel in the first place.

------------------------------------------------- BEAT 05 — MISCONCEPTION TWO

Number two. Not all jinn are evil.

Surah Al-Jinn, verse eleven — and this is the jinn speaking about themselves:
and among us are the righteous, and among us are others below that. We follow
different paths.

They have believers and disbelievers. Some get it right. Some don't. Exactly
like us.

Two verses later: and when we heard the guidance, we believed in it.

So jinn is not another word for shaytan. Shaytan describes behaviour, not
species. [pause] And that turns out to apply to more of us than people expect.

------------------------------------------ BEAT 06 — THE NIGHT THEY LISTENED

Which brings us to how the Quran says they were created — and why.

Surah Adh-Dhariyat, verse fifty-six: and I did not create the jinn and mankind
except to worship Me.

One sentence. Both named together. Which is why Surah Ar-Rahman keeps addressing
two audiences and asking, thirty-one times, which of your Lord's favours you
would deny.

And then Surah Al-Jinn opens with something remarkable. Say: it has been
revealed to me that a group of the jinn listened — and they said, indeed, we
have heard an amazing Quran.

Surah Al-Ahqaf tells the same night from their side. They listened. They told

warners: O our people, respond to the caller of Allah.

Nobody summoned them. No ritual. They passed by, they heard recitation, and it
moved them.

Now — what can they actually do?

The Quran answers that through Sulayman, peace be upon him.

Surah An-Naml: gathered for Sulayman were his soldiers of the jinn and men and

He died leaning on his staff. And the jinn working under him kept working —

Surah Saba, verse fourteen: when he fell, it became clear to the jinn that if

Number four.

Surah Al-Jinn, verse six: and there were men from mankind who sought refuge in

That was a real practice. Travellers would stop somewhere unfamiliar and ask the

They asked for protection. They received weight.

[serious] Seeking safety from the concealed does not produce safety. It produces

From among the jinn. And from among mankind.

[pause] The final line of the Quran names a human threat standing right beside

Which means shaytan was never a species. It's a job description. And some of

in comments, cite 18:50 and leave the classical dispute alone — you do not need
to win it and engaging invites a thread you cannot close.

"THEY CANNOT KNOW THE UNSEEN" — Saba 34:14 is the strongest verse in the video

and al-Raghib al-Isfahani's Mufradat. Attribute it as "the root means," never as
"the Quran says." That distinction is the difference between a scholarly channel
and one that gets corrected publicly.
