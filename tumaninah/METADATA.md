## Description

---

The āyah people put on their wall lives inside a sūrah named after the loudest
thing in the sky. And the Arabic underneath "hearts find rest" is doing something
far more physical than the English lets on.

Ṭumaʾnīnah does not mean peace, and it does not mean calm. Its root sense is to
settle — to sink down and come to rest, the way silt drops out of stirred water,
the way ground finishes moving. It is the same word the Prophet ﷺ used to correct
a man's prayer: bow until you are settled. A prayer without it is not a prayer.

This is a slow walk through Ar-Raʿd 27–28 — where the line sits, what the
sentence before it does to it, why the second half is the half that matters, and
where the same root turns up at the very end of the Qurʾān as something other
than a verb.
