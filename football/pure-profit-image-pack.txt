Model-agnostic — ElevenLabs flows, or any image model. Every frame is a
still, moved in the edit; the MOVE line is the entire animation. A still
pushed slowly for ten seconds is indistinguishable from a generated clip
and has no temporal artefacts, at a fraction of the cost.

THE RHYME. One rule holds the set together:

    ACCOUNTING OBJECTS CARRY THE MECHANISM.
    THE GROUND CARRIES THE BOY.

Ledger, light, paper, calendar and clock argue the case. Pitch, locker,
tunnel and kit bag are the person it happens to. They alternate act by
act, and they collide once — at frame 21.

PALETTE. Overcast morning, wet grass, low sun behind cloud. Desaturated
greens, cold greys, one warm lamp. A training ground at 7am and an office
after hours — never a matchday. Photographic throughout, shallow depth of
field, no illustration anywhere.

ANCHOR. GENERATE FRAME 16 FIRST. Reuse its seed on 01, 31, 32 and 33 so
the ground, weather and hour hold across five minutes.

RIGHTS. No club badge or crest, no competition logo, no sponsor or
manufacturer mark, no nameset or shirt number, no recognisable player or
likeness, no identifiable stadium, nothing resembling broadcast footage.
The anonymity is not an aesthetic that happens to be safe — it is the
safety, and it happens to be the aesthetic.

NEGATIVE PROMPT — paste once, applies to all 33 frames:

facial features, eyes, mouth, nose, lips, face, portrait, club badge,
crest, emblem, sponsor logo, kit manufacturer logo, competition logo,
nameset, shirt number, recognisable footballer, celebrity likeness,
identifiable stadium, broadcast overlay, scoreboard graphics, text,
lettering, numbers, logos, brand names, watermark, signature, cartoon
outlines, cel shading, flat vector, anime, 2D illustration, distorted
hands, extra fingers, extra limbs, camera shake, blurry, low resolution,
oversaturated, plastic sheen, uncanny, stock-photo smiles, crowd
celebration, confetti

NUMBERS are burned in during the edit, never generated. Each frame's
burn-in is listed with it. Frames marked (none) carry no text at all —
they are the breathing room, and the film needs them.
