[serious] They sold the academy kid.

Not because he wasn't good enough. Not because he asked to leave. They
sold him because of where his name sat on a spreadsheet.

Ten years at the club. Every age group. Two loans. A season where somebody
finally decided he was ready.

Forty appearances... and then a fee, an announcement, and a timeline full

### ACT TWO — HOW A SIGNING IS BOOKED   [0:48.0-2:22.0 · 94.0s]   1108 chars
------------------------------------------------------------------------
--- START ---
A club doesn't see a squad. It sees a column of numbers, and every one of
them shrinks a little each year.

Start with a signing. Fifty million pounds. Five-year contract. That is
not a fifty million pound cost in the year you buy him.

The fee is spread across the length of the deal. Ten million a year, for
five years. Accountants call it amortisation.

[curious] It is the most important word in modern football, and almost
nobody says it out loud.

Year one, ten million comes off. Year two, ten more. What's left is what
he is still worth on paper. His book value.

Two years in, twenty million written off. Thirty million left on the
books — and he hasn't played any differently.

Now — spread the same fee over eight years instead of five, and the annual
cost drops from ten million to six. Clubs noticed.

So the rules changed. Five years is the cap now. That change happened
because of exactly this.

Now sell him. Forty million pounds. [serious] But the fee is not the
profit. Only the gap above book value is.

Forty million, minus thirty. Ten million of profit... on a player you sold
for forty.
--- END ---

### ACT FIVE — WHY IT HAPPENS IN JUNE   [3:46.0-4:46.0 · 60.0s]   622 chars
------------------------------------------------------------------------
--- START ---
Now put a deadline on it. Every club files accounts. Profit and
sustainability rules measure what is in them.

The Premier League lets a club lose one hundred and five million pounds
across three seasons. Cross it, and the punishment is the table itself.

Two clubs found that out. Points gone, mid-season, over numbers filed a
year earlier.

So in the last week of June, a club doesn't need a player. [serious] It
needs a figure.

And when you need a figure fast, you don't sell the thirty million pound
asset for a ten million gain.

You sell the one whose entire fee lands as profit — on the right side of
the deadline.
--- END ---
