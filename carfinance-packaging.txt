4. DESCRIPTION
================================================================================

Structure copied from the reference video: links first, then mechanism prose,
then the check, then the series index, then the disclaimer. 600+ characters
minimum — do not ship the 197-character boilerplate.

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  ▶ Part 13 — how to buy a car without interest (Ijara): https://youtu.be/ElD5gg-Q1wY
  ▶ Part 1 — how banks rent out money and call it interest: https://youtu.be/V8HYpTHy2aU
  ▶ FULL BREAKDOWN — the whole machine in 5 minutes: https://youtu.be/7TWUwpbl83U

  Three years of payments, and then you give the car back. That is not the
  problem. Renting a car is trade, and a lease has a name in Islamic finance:
  ijara. Not owning the car is fine.

  Here is what isn't.

  A PCP splits the car into two parts: the part you pay off over the term, and
  a lump deferred to the very end — the optional final payment, the balloon,
  the Guaranteed Minimum Future Value. Most people never pay the balloon.
  Handing the car back is the normal exit; it is what the product is built
  around.

  But the balloon sits inside the financed amount for the whole term, and
  interest is charged on all of it. On a £30,000 car with £3,000 down and a
  £13,000 balloon at around 10% over three years, that is roughly £103 a month
  — about £3,700 by the end, or nearly two-thirds of the total interest on the
  agreement — charged on a slab of car you were always going to walk away from.
  That is why the monthly looks affordable and the total charge for credit
  doesn't.

  Run the test from Part 1: does the contract charge you for time? Every month,
  on £13,000 of car that will never be yours, it does. And that charge doesn't
  depend on the car. It depends on the calendar.

  Worth knowing, though it is not the video's argument: on a PCP the finance
  company is the legal owner for the whole term. You are the registered keeper
  — the person the DVLA writes to — and the V5C says in terms that it is not
  proof of ownership. Keeper and owner were never meant to be the same thing.

  THE TWO-MINUTE CHECK:

  1. Find the optional final payment on your agreement. It is on page one, in
     its own box, and it is usually the largest single number on the document.
  2. Find the total amount payable and the total charge for credit. Subtract
     the cash price of the car. What is left is what time cost you.
  3. Decide honestly which one you are. If you intend to hand it back you are
     renting — so compare it against a straight lease, where you are not
     financing a balloon you will never pay. If you intend to keep it you are
     buying — so compare the total charge for credit against saving for it.

  Separately, and worth two minutes of its own: the FCA's motor finance redress
  scheme covers PCP and HP agreements taken out between 6 April 2007 and
  1 November 2024 where commission arrangements weren't properly disclosed.
  Around 12.1 million agreements are in scope. It is free to use and you do not
  need a claims company. Check the FCA's own site for where it currently
  stands — parts of it have been subject to legal challenge.

  That scheme is about disclosure. This video is about the structure
  underneath it, which no redress scheme touches.

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Two things worth saying before anyone panics. Handing the car back at the end
  is not a loophole or a failure — it is the normal exit and the product is
  built around it. And if you have already signed one, the useful move isn't
  guilt, it's arithmetic: find the total charge for credit, subtract nothing,

  An HP has no optional final payment. A PCP does.

7. THE NICHE BENCHMARK — and its limits, stated honestly
================================================================================

I ran an outlier sweep on car finance and balloon-payment Shorts earlier in this
session and it returned mostly noise: "balloon" pulled ASMR balloon-crushing
videos and "debt" pulled entertainment clips. Two things did come through and
both are worth knowing.

  THE DAVE RAMSEY CLIP ECOSYSTEM OWNS DEBT SHORTS. One clip channel had 7.1M
  views on a single car-debt Short. That format is confrontation and reaction —
  a caller, a number, an incredulous host. It is structurally unavailable to
  you and you should not try to imitate it. Do not read those view counts as a
  target.

  THE ONE USABLE STRUCTURAL COMPARABLE was "Car Loan vs Cash Payment" — 688k
  views, 65 seconds, from a 176k-subscriber channel. It is a calculation
  explainer: one worked example, real numbers, no personality. That is the same
  genre as yours, and it confirms the thing your own data already says — the
  numbers are the content. It is also the reason the rewritten script has a
  worked example in it at all, which the original draft did not.

BEYOND THAT, THE HONEST ANSWER IS THAT YOUR OWN CHANNEL IS THE BETTER
BENCHMARK. Twenty-five Shorts, one audience, one format, a clean split between
a winning title convention and a losing one, and a same-week natural experiment
in Pension versus Klarna. Cross-niche title patterns from channels with 100x
your subscriber count would tell you less, because their titles are carried by
an existing audience and yours have to work cold.
