v1 of this file audited the emotion tags in isolation and rewrote them. That

2. THE REFERENCE — what Part 8 actually does
================================================================================

Part 8 is 3:00, 1,231 views, and a 5.2% like rate, which is exceptional. Here
is its machinery, cross-checked against Part 13 (Ijara), which runs the same
build. Both share nine recurring devices. The PCP draft uses none of them.

  D1  COUNTER-INTUITIVE HOOK.
      P8: "imagine there's a mortgage where your debt gets smaller while you
      sleep." P13: "You don't pay for the car. You pay rent on the money that
      bought the car. And that rent never sleeps."
      The "while you sleep" motif recurs across the series. It is the channel's
      signature image and it is free to reuse.

  D2  SERIES RECAP IN THE FIRST 25 SECONDS.
      "Quick recap if you're new here. In part one... in part six..."
      Establishes authority and sells the back catalogue before the argument
      starts. The credit card pack claimed this channel has no series framing.
      That is out of date — the series runs to Part 14 with a playlist, and the
      taught videos all open on it.

  D3  A WORKED EXAMPLE WITH REAL NUMBERS.
      P13: "$30,000 at 9% for 6 years, 540 a month. By the end you've paid
      nearly 39,000. That extra 9,000 never touched the car."
      Every winning video has one. The PCP draft has zero numbers in it. This
      is the single largest content gap.

  D4  A TURN SIGNAL BEFORE THE REVEAL.
      "And here's where it gets good." / "here's the part nobody says out loud."

  D5  FIRST-PERSON DISCOVERY.
      "When someone first explained this to me, I honestly thought they were
      joking. They weren't." / "when someone first showed me the fix, I thought
      it was too simple to be real."
      The narrator learns alongside the viewer. This is the warmth in the
      format, and it is what makes a riba video feel like teaching rather than
      accusation. The PCP draft is pure second-person accusation — it tells the
      viewer what they did, fourteen times, and never once stands next to them.

  D6  "IT'S OVER A THOUSAND YEARS OLD."

  D7  THE SERIES TEST. The signature device.
      P8: "If the roof caves in tomorrow, who pays? Both of us."
      P13: "If the engine dies tomorrow, does the lender lose anything?
      Nothing."
      There is also an explicit three-question version the channel pinned in
      its own comments: does the contract charge for time / is the fee flat or
      does it scale / where does a late fee go. Use question one below.

  D8  QURAN 2:275, LATE, AS THE SEAL. "Trade permitted, riba forbidden."

  D9  NEXT-PART TEASE, THEN "NO JARGON, JUST MECHANISMS."
      Every episode. The draft's ending is a bare rhetorical question and does
      not sign off.

THE DELIVERY REGISTER — and this is the correction to v1. The shot-by-shot
read of Part 8 describes the VO as "calming, intrigue-focused, almost
whisper-soft," "serene and intimate, ASMR-adjacent warmth," delivered in a
lower register for an insider-secret feel. Its punch beats are not loud. They
are pitch DROPS into flat certainty:

    0:10  "They weren't."
    0:41  "This is it."
    2:16  "Both of us."

That is the opposite of what v1 built. v1 added [angry] on the injustice beat
on general viral-craft logic. On this channel that is off-voice — the format's
authority comes from being the calmest person in the room while describing
something outrageous. [angry] is cut from the script below and should not come
back.

and pointed at Part 13; it is never re-explained.

WHAT SURVIVES AS NEW. Part 13 covered a straight amortising car loan. A PCP is
a different mechanism — most of the car is never amortised at all, it is parked
in a balloon and charged for anyway. That is genuinely unclaimed, it is the
most common car finance product in the UK, and it is worth its own episode.
Slot it as its own part number and open on Part 13 in the recap.

[serious] Three years of payments. Then you hand the car back.

And here's the strange part. You paid interest on the piece of it you were
always going to give away.

[calm] Quick recap if you're new here. Part one — banks rent out money and call
it interest. Part thirteen — we put that on a car, and found the fix. Ijara.

Today it's the deal most of Britain actually drives. P-C-P.

Say the car is thirty thousand. You put down three. So twenty-seven thousand is
financed, at around ten percent, over three years.

But a PCP doesn't finance all of it the same way. [curious] And this is the bit
nobody explains at the desk.

Thirteen thousand pounds of that car gets set aside. Parked at the end of the
contract, untouched. Your monthly payments never reduce it. Not by a penny.

That's the balloon. The optional final payment. The one you were always going
to walk away from.

[curious] So if you're not paying it down... what are you paying on it?

[whispers] Interest. Every single month.

Thirteen thousand pounds, sitting there, charged at ten percent, for
thirty-six months. About a hundred and three pounds a month. Three thousand
seven hundred pounds by the end.

[serious] Nearly two-thirds of all the interest on that contract. Charged on a
piece of car you never intended to own, and never did.

[calm] Now run the test from part one. Does the contract charge you for time?

Every month, on thirteen thousand pounds of car that will never be yours — yes.
It does. And that charge doesn't depend on the car. It depends on the calendar.

[curious] But hold on. Isn't a PCP just a lease? And leasing is fine, isn't it?

That was my first question too. And renting a car is trade — that's Ijara,
that's part thirteen. But a lease doesn't charge you for a share you're handing
back. Under Ijara the owner owns the car, carries it, and rents you a thing.

[serious] Under a PCP you carry the cost of owning it, and you hold the rights
of renting it. Two contracts wearing each other's clothes.

Trade permitted, riba forbidden. Surah Al-Baqarah, two two seven five.

[whispers] Go and find your balloon. It's on page one of your agreement —
"optional final payment."

[warmly] Then ask the only question that matters. What am I actually renting?
And what am I paying for time?

No jargon. Just mechanisms.

================================================================================
5. v3 MECHANICS — the tag findings that still hold from v1
================================================================================

Settings: stability 45–55, similarity 75, speed 1.0. Above ~60 v3 begins
ignoring tags entirely; if the whispers are coming out as ordinary narration
that is almost always the cause.

5.1  TAGS WORK BY CONTRAST, NOT AS SETTINGS. The original draft ran [serious]
     three times out of four tags. On a voice already narrating a riba
     explainer that describes the baseline and instructs nothing. Above,
     [serious] appears only where something quieter or more curious precedes
     it, so it has a delta to express.

5.2  CONSECUTIVE DUPLICATE TAGS ARE NO-OPS. The draft's two [warmly] in a row —
     v3 holds a state until contradicted, so the second changed nothing and
     cost you the CTA differentiation. Fixed above by whispering the
     instruction and warming only the final question.

5.3  NON-CANONICAL TAGS ARE SOFT. [serious], [calm] and [warmly] are not in
     ElevenLabs' documented v3 set. v3 is prompt-following so they do something,
     but weakly and inconsistently. [whispers] and [curious] are documented and
     steer hard — which is why the four load-bearing beats above sit on those
     two and the mood tags only carry connective tissue.

5.4  TAGS ON SHORT LINES DON'T LAND. v3 ramps into a state over roughly a line.
     "[whispers] Interest. Every single month." is deliberately two sentences,
     not one, so the whisper has somewhere to arrive.

5.5  GENERATE IN ONE PASS. v3 wants ~250+ characters to hold a state; recording
     line by line gives you twenty performances of the same voice.

5.6  IF A TAG IS AUDIBLY SPOKEN, the generation is scrap. Listen end to end
     before you cut to it.

5.7  STRIP ORDER if the take sounds theatrical: [curious] on the lease
     objection first, then [calm] on the recap, then the third [serious].
     Never strip the two [whispers] or the closing [warmly].

PRONUNCIATION.
  P-C-P — hyphenated in the script text to force letter-by-letter. Unhyphenated
  v3 will eventually say "pee-see-pee" as a word, and PCP has an unfortunate
  other referent your comments will find.
  IJARA — v3 has no phoneme tags; respelling in the script is the only lever.
  Expect "eye-JAR-uh" first pass. If you get it, write "ee-JAA-ra" and v3 reads
  what you wrote. Note Part 13's title spells it "Ijara" and its VO says
  "Ijarah" — pick one for the series and stay on it.
  2:275 — written "two two seven five" above so v3 doesn't read it as a
  timestamp or a ratio.

6. THE NUMBERS — check these against a real quote before recording
================================================================================

Worked from a representative UK PCP: £30,000 car, £3,000 deposit, £27,000
financed, ~10% APR, 36 months, £13,000 guaranteed minimum future value.

  Monthly payment            ~£552
  Total paid over the term   ~£22,872  (deposit + 36 payments)
  Total interest             ~£5,872   (if you go on to buy at the balloon)
  Interest on the balloon    ~£3,700   (£13,000 × ~0.79%/mo × 36)
  Balloon share of interest  ~63%

"Nearly two-thirds" in the VO is that 63%. A £13,000 GMFV on a £30,000 car at
three years is ~43% of list, which is squarely in the normal band for a
3-year/10k-mile PCP. All of it is illustrative, none of it names a lender, and
the VO says "say the car is thirty thousand" so it never claims to be a
specific product. Same posture as the credit card pack's 1%-vs-24.9% beat.

Burn these on screen, do not generate them: 30,000 / 3,000 / 13,000 / £103 A
MONTH / £3,700 / 63%. The £103-vs-£3,700 collision is this video's equivalent
of "£1,461 a month, only £420 buys your house" — give it the most typographic
weight in the film.

7. ACCURACY — three things that have to survive the comments
================================================================================

7.1  THE LOGBOOK LINE IS WRONG AND IS CUT.
     The draft said "on a PCP the logbook was never in your name." On a UK PCP
     the V5C normally IS in the customer's name — they are the registered
     keeper; the finance company is the legal owner, and the V5C says in terms
     that it is not proof of ownership. Anyone who has held one for a financed
     car can check this in thirty seconds. Gone from the script entirely.

7.2  DO NOT SAY THE LENDER CARRIES NO RISK.
     This is the trap in porting Part 13's risk test straight across. On a
     straight car loan "if the engine dies, does the lender lose anything?
     Nothing" is fair. On a PCP it is not: the lender GUARANTEES the minimum
     future value, so if the car is worth less than the balloon at hand-back
     the lender eats that difference. That is a real risk they take, and a
     knowledgeable commenter will say so and be right.
     The script above therefore runs a different test — your own question one,
     "does the contract charge for time?" — which is unarguable here and is
     the actual riba claim. Keep it narrow. The argument is that you are
     charged for time on a portion you will never own, not that the lender is
     risk-free.

7.3  INTEREST ON THE BALLOON IS THE CENTRAL CLAIM AND IT IS SOUND.
     Interest on a PCP is charged on the whole amount financed, including the
     optional final payment, for the entire term. A customer who always
     intended to hand the car back has still paid interest on the balloon
     every month. This is the video and it holds.

POSTURE, unchanged from both published packs: describe the economics, do not
issue a ruling in your own voice. Ijara is named as the contrast, not as a
verdict.

8. PACKAGING — do not repeat the August batch's mistake
================================================================================

The six videos doing 24–50 views all have bare declarative titles with no
keywords and no hashtags. The six doing 700–1,200 all follow "How to X (Y
Explained)" or "Is Your X Halal?" with three trailing hashtags. That pattern is
the cheapest thing on this list to copy.

  TITLE   How to Buy a Car on PCP Without Riba (The Balloon Explained)
          #pcp #carfinance #islamicfinance
    alt   Is Your Car Finance Halal? (You're Paying Interest on a Car You Give
          Back)
    alt   The Part of Your Car Finance Nobody Explains (PCP Balloon Explained)

  Do not ship it as "The Balloon." or "Three Years. Then You Hand It Back."
  That is the naming convention on every video in the failing block.

  LENGTH  2:10–2:30. Not 50 seconds. The 17–58s cuts are the worst-performing
          content on the channel by a wide margin.
  SERIES  Give it a part number, open the recap on Part 13, and add it to the
          playlist — a viewer already flagged in the comments that a missing
          playlist entry broke the run order for them.
  TEASE   Close the description with the next part, as every winning episode
          does.
  PINNED  Pin the comment hook at upload. The channel does this every time and
          the format is settled: a question the viewer can answer from their
          own paperwork. Use —
            "Go and look at your agreement tonight. What's your optional final
            payment — and what percentage of the car is it? 👇"
          That is the same shape as "Run them tonight. What did yours score?"
          and it is designed to farm the exact comment the algorithm rewards.
