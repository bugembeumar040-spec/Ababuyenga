WHAT THIS FILE IS

The PCP / balloon-payment VO, audited against the v3 tagging conventions used
on the Klarna and credit card packs, and rewritten. Section 1 is the audit —
what the draft was doing wrong. Section 2 is the script to record. Sections
3–6 are the settings, the strip order if v3 over-performs, the two
pronunciation traps, and one accuracy fix that has nothing to do with tags but
would have ended the video in the comments.

The scene map, captions and prompt pack for this video do not exist yet. Record
this first, then derive the beats off the rendered audio — same rule as the
credit card pack.


================================================================================
1. AUDIT — what the draft was doing
================================================================================

The draft carried four tags across fourteen lines: [serious] ×3, [calm],
[warmly] ×2. Eleven findings, roughly in order of how much they cost you.

1.1  NO CONTRAST, SO NO EMOTION.
     v3 does not read a tag as an absolute setting. It reads it as a move away
     from the surrounding delivery. [serious] on a voice already narrating a
     riba explainer at stability 50 is a null instruction — it describes the
     baseline. Three of the four tags in the draft were [serious]. The script
     was effectively untagged.

1.2  THE HOOK HAD NO DELIVERY DIRECTION.
     "Three years of payments. Then you hand the car back." is the whole
     retention decision and it was carrying the same tag as the middle of the
     script. The Klarna and credit card packs both open cold-flat and drop to
     [whispers] on the reveal. This draft never uses the device.

1.3  THE SINGLE-WORD BOMB WAS UNTAGGED.
     "The balloon." is this video's "Deadbeats." — the two-word line the whole
     film is built to land. It sat in the middle of a [serious] block with no
     isolation and no tag. That is the one place a tag was mandatory and the
     one place there wasn't one.

1.4  CONSECUTIVE DUPLICATE TAGS ARE NO-OPS.
     [warmly] on the last two lines. The second one changes nothing — v3 holds
     an emotional state until the next tag contradicts it. You spent a tag to
     instruct the model to keep doing what it was already doing, and lost the
     chance to differentiate the CTA from the line before it.

1.5  NON-CANONICAL TAG VOCABULARY.
     [serious], [calm], [warmly] and [emphatic] are not in ElevenLabs' v3 tag
     documentation. v3 is prompt-following rather than enum-driven so they do
     something, but the effect is soft and it varies run to run. The documented
     set — [whispers], [sighs], [exhales], [curious], [sarcastic], [excited],
     [angry], [laughs], [shouts] — steers far harder for the same token cost.
     Rewrite below keeps three soft mood tags where the soft effect is what's
     wanted and moves every load-bearing beat onto the documented set.

1.6  TAGS ON SHORT LINES DON'T LAND.
     v3 ramps into an emotional state over roughly a line. A tag in front of a
     four-word sentence is often spent before the sentence is over. Applies to
     "Here's the part that isn't." and "The balloon." Fix is either to tag the
     line before and let the state carry in, or to isolate the beat as its own
     generation. Both used below.

1.7  NO PACING MARKS AT ALL.
     Full stops were doing 100% of the work. v3 rushes list-shaped prose, and
     "So there are two flows. Money leaving you, like a buyer's. And a logbook
     that never moves, like a tenant's." is the most list-shaped passage in the
     script — it will come out flat and fast, exactly on the beat you need the
     viewer to actually think. Em-dashes and ellipses steer pace more reliably
     than any tag; the rewrite uses them.

1.8  CHUNKS TOO SHORT FOR STABLE EMOTION.
     v3 wants ~250+ characters per generation to hold a state. Recording this
     line by line will give you fourteen different performances of the same
     voice. Generate as one pass, with exactly one exception (§5).

1.9  THE ANALYTICAL BEAT HAS NO EMOTION AVAILABLE TO IT.
     "Two flows... you carry the cost of owning it and you hold the rights of
     someone renting it" is the argument, and it's the driest sentence in the
     script. No tag fixes abstract copy. Rewritten below into second person and
     split into two short opposed clauses so the delivery has something to
     push against.

1.10 THE ARC WAS FLAT.
     serious → calm → serious → warm → warm. What the two published packs
     actually do, and what the rewrite restores: cold open, drop to whisper on
     the reveal, controlled heat on the injustice, drop again on the
     instruction, warm only on the last line.

1.11 NO CTA DIFFERENTIATION.
     "Go and find the balloon. It's on page one." is an instruction — it should
     be quiet and close-miked, not warm. Warmth belongs on the final line only,
     where it's the payoff for 45 seconds of pressure. The draft gave both
     lines the same tag and flattened the ending.


================================================================================
2. THE SCRIPT — record this
================================================================================

Tag count: 8 across the film, six of them documented v3 tags. Punctuation
carries the pace; tags are reserved for the four moves that matter.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[serious] Three years of payments. Then you hand the car back.

[whispers] You never owned it. Not for one day of it.

[calm] And that part is fine. A lease is a lease — Islam has a name for it.
Ijara. Nothing wrong with renting a car.

[serious] Here's the part that isn't.

The balloon. That lump at the end — the one you were always going to walk away
from.

You pay interest on that too. Every month. For three years.

[angry] Interest... on a piece of car you were never going to own.

[curious] So which one are you?

The money leaves you like a buyer. The keys go back like a tenant.
You carried the cost of owning it. You held the rights of renting it.

[whispers] Go and find the balloon. It's on page one of your agreement.

[warmly] Then ask what you're actually renting — and what you're paying for
time.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT EACH TAG IS DOING

  [serious]   Baseline. Stated once at the top so the whisper two lines later
              has something to be a departure from. Restated after [calm] to
              snap back — that one is load-bearing, it's the pivot of the film.
  [whispers]  The reveal. Drops on "You never owned it" and carries into the
              short line after it, which is why the two sentences are in one
              chunk rather than split.
  [calm]      The concession. Deliberately soft — this is the beat where a
              hostile viewer decides you're being fair, and it buys you the
              rest of the video.
  [angry]     The injustice, once. Do not add a second one anywhere. Its entire
              power is that it's the only raised moment in fifty seconds. If
              your voice build over-performs it, see §4.
  [curious]   Turns the analytical beat into a question aimed at the viewer.
              This is the tag doing the most work per token in the script — it
              converts the driest passage into direct address.
  [whispers]  Second whisper, on the instruction. Close-miked, conspiratorial.
              Pairs with the first whisper to bracket the argument.
  [warmly]    Last line only. The only warmth in the film.

"The balloon." is untagged on purpose — it inherits [serious] from the pivot
and lands on the silence around it, not on a performance. See §5.


================================================================================
3. SETTINGS
================================================================================

ElevenLabs v3, stability 45–55, similarity 75, speed 1.0. Same as Klarna and
the credit card pack — do not change these to chase the tags.

Stability is the thing that decides whether tags work at all. Above ~60 v3
starts ignoring them; at 45–55 they land and the voice still holds identity.
If [angry] and [whispers] are coming out as ordinary narration, the problem is
almost always stability drifting high, not the tags.

Generate the whole script as one pass. Fourteen separate generations will not
match.


================================================================================
4. STRIP ORDER — if v3 over-performs
================================================================================

Eight tags is more than the two published packs use and it is deliberate, but
v3 occasionally reads an over-tagged script as a performance rather than a
narration. If the take sounds theatrical, remove in this order and regenerate
after each:

  1. [curious]   — the question mark alone gets you most of it
  2. [calm]      — the copy is already conciliatory
  3. [serious] (the second one, at "Here's the part that isn't")
  4. [angry]     — replace with the ellipsis alone, which is already in the line

Never strip the two [whispers] or the closing [warmly]. Those three are the
film's entire dynamic range; without them you have a man reading a leaflet.

Separately: if a tag is ever audibly spoken in the take — v3 does this
occasionally — it is instantly obvious and the whole generation is scrap. Listen
to every pass end to end before you cut to it.


================================================================================
5. THE ISOLATED BEAT
================================================================================

"The balloon." is this video's "Deadbeats." Same treatment as the credit card
pack: a full beat of silence either side in the edit, and nothing riding in on
it.

If v3 rushes it in the full pass — likely, it's three words after a long
sentence — generate the pivot on its own:

    [serious] Here's the part that isn't. [long pause] The balloon.

and cut that in. Do not solve it by adding a tag to "The balloon." in the main
pass; per §1.6 the state won't ramp in time and you'll get an odd half-inflected
read instead.


================================================================================
6. PRONUNCIATION — two traps
================================================================================

v3 does not support SSML phoneme or alias tags. The only lever is respelling in
the script text itself, so test both of these before you commit to a take.

  IJARA — Arabic term, and v3 has no reason to know it. Expect a hard English
  "eye-JAR-uh" on first pass. If you get it, respell it in the script as
  "Ijaara" or, if that still fails, "ee-JAA-ra" — v3 reads the respelling, so
  whatever you write is what you hear. Get this one right; mispronouncing the
  Islamic term while explaining Islamic finance is the single most quotable
  error available in this script.

  PCP — the draft's second line contained it and the rewrite removes it, partly
  for this reason. If you put it back, write it "P-C-P" with hyphens to force
  letter-by-letter. Unhyphenated, v3 will sooner or later say "pee-see-pee" as
  a word, and PCP also has an unfortunate other referent your comment section
  will find funny.


================================================================================
7. ACCURACY — one fix that is not a tag problem
================================================================================

THE LOGBOOK LINE IS WRONG AND HAD TO GO.

The draft read: "On a PCP the logbook was never in your name."

On a UK PCP or HP agreement the V5C registration document normally IS in the
customer's name — the customer is the registered keeper. The finance company is
the legal owner. The V5C itself carries the line that it is not proof of
ownership, and that distinction between keeper and owner is exactly the thing
the document exists to make. Anyone who has held a V5C for a financed car — a
large share of your audience — can check this in thirty seconds, and it is the
kind of error that gets a top comment and discredits the argument around it.

The rewrite replaces the whole keeper/owner mechanic with the flow the script
can defend without qualification: money out like a buyer, keys back like a
tenant. Same point, no falsifiable claim.

Keep the rest of the argument as drafted. Interest on a PCP is charged on the
full amount financed including the optional final payment, so a customer who
always intended to hand the car back has still been paying interest on the
balloon for the whole term — that is the video's central claim and it is sound.

Posture, as in both published packs: describe the economics, do not issue a
ruling in your own voice. Ijara is named as the contrast, not as a verdict.
