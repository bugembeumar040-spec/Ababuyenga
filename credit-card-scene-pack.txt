THE ANGLE THAT CONTINUES KLARNA INSTEAD:
Your Klarna video's thesis is in its title — "You've Already Paid Klarna."
The point being that the merchant fee is baked into the shelf price, so the
cost reaches you whether or not you ever open the app.

Credit cards are the same mechanism, one layer deeper, and almost nobody in
this niche has covered it:

  • Every card sale costs the merchant a fee (interchange + scheme fee +
    acquirer margin = the merchant service charge).
  • Merchants do not run two price lists. They price the fee into the shelf
    price for everybody.
  • Therefore the cash payer and the debit payer fund the credit card system
    too — they simply collect none of the rewards.
  • Those rewards — cashback, air miles, lounge access — are funded out of
    that same fee. So the reward flows to the person with the premium card,
    paid for by the person who has none.
  • That is a regressive transfer, and it is documented (see PART 8).
  • Only THEN the riba point: the interest term sits in the contract from the
    day you sign, whether or not you ever trigger it.

Why this works for your channel specifically:
  - It is a genuine mechanism reveal, which is your top-tier pattern.
  - It has the two-numbers-in-collision hook you already use.
  - It does not repeat the 198-view video.
  - It sequels your newest upload directly, so it builds a series.
  - The way out is murabaha/debit — and you already have a murabaha video to
    point at, which is free retention for an older upload.

PART 8 — FACT-CHECK: EVERY NUMBER IN THIS SCRIPT
===============================================================================

"The shop keeps about ninety-eight" (~2% merchant service charge)
  Defensible for a SMALL UK merchant on a blended rate. Typical UK small-
  business all-in card cost runs roughly 1.5–2% once scheme fees and acquirer
  margin sit on top of interchange. Blended providers advertise around 1.75%.
  CAVEATS YOU SHOULD KNOW BEFORE SOMEONE POSTS THEM:
    - UK/EU consumer credit card INTERCHANGE alone is capped at 0.3% (0.2%
      debit). The ~2% is the merchant's TOTAL cost, not interchange. The
      script says "the shop keeps about ninety-eight" and never says the word
      interchange in the VO — keep it that way, it is the accurate framing.
    - Large retailers negotiate materially lower rates.
    - Amex typically costs the merchant more.
    - Commercial cards and non-UK-issued cards are not capped.
  If you want to be bulletproof, say "about ninety-eight" not "exactly
  ninety-eight". The script already does.

"Federal Reserve Bank of Boston... cash payers subsidise card rewards, money
moves from lower income to higher income households"
  Real. Schuh, Shy & Stavins, "Who Gains and Who Loses from Credit Card
  Payments? Theory and Calibrations", FRB Boston Public Policy Discussion
  Paper No. 10-03, 2010. Its headline calibration is that the average cash-
  paying household transfers ~$151/year to card-paying households, and the
  average card-paying household receives ~$1,482/year, with a regressive
  income gradient.
  IT IS US DATA AND IT IS FROM 2010. The on-screen text labels it as such.
  Do not present it as UK or as current. If you want to quote the dollar
  figures on screen, label them "US, 2010" too.

"The interest term is in the contract from the day you sign it"
  Accurate as a description of a conventional credit card agreement — the
  interest provision exists in the agreement regardless of whether a balance
  is ever carried. Note the script states this as a FACT ABOUT THE CONTRACT
  and does not issue a ruling. That is deliberate: contemporary scholars
  genuinely differ on cards where interest is never incurred, and the pinned
  comment handles it honestly rather than pretending the difference away.
  Don't "strengthen" this line into a verdict.

WHAT THIS SCRIPT DELIBERATELY DOES NOT DO
  It does not repeat minimum payments, APR, or compounding. That is your
  198-view video. Every second here is spent on the mechanism you have not
  covered.
