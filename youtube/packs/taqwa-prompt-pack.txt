WHAT THIS FILE IS

One video, three aligned tracks, built off a single beat map:

  TRACK 1 — STILLS.   14 generated images, Flow Labs, moved slowly in the edit.
  TRACK 2 — TYPE.     Arabic + citation + translation, TYPESET, never generated.
  TRACK 3 — VOICE.    ElevenLabs v3 script with emotion tags, timed to the map.

Every track is keyed to the same beat numbers. If you cut a beat, cut it in all
three or the film desynchronises — the Arabic panel is the hard sync point,
because it is the one element the viewer reads rather than hears.

THE ONE RULE THAT GOVERNS EVERYTHING BELOW. No Arabic is ever generated by an
image model. Not a word, not a flourish, not a background texture that resembles
script. Every image prompt asks for lettering-free artwork on purpose, and the
negative prompt enforces it. The Arabic is typeset in the edit from a verified
mushaf. A hallucinated letter in a Quranic verse is the one mistake this channel
cannot survive, and image models hallucinate Arabic every single time.

--- 01 · THE MISTRANSLATION · budget 67 · actual 54 ------------------------------
[serious] Taqwa gets translated as fear of God. Almost every English Quran does it.
And the word does not come from any root that means fear.
Arabic has words for fear. Several of them. This is not one.
[thoughtful] The root of taqwa means to guard. To put something between yourself and what can harm you.

--- 02 · THE ROOT · budget 77 · actual 66 ----------------------------------------
The root is waw, qaf, ya. W-Q-Y.
From it comes wiqayah — a shield, a covering, the thing you place in the way.
A wiqayah is what you hold up. It is an object, and an action.
Taqwa is built directly on it. Not on khawf. Not on khashyah. Not on rahbah.
[serious] So the question isn't how afraid you are. It's what you've put in the way.

--- 03 · THE PROOF VERB · budget 82 · actual 80 ----------------------------------
The clearest proof is a verse most people have heard translated softly.
Surah At-Tahrim, verse six. Qu anfusakum wa ahlikum naran.
   >> ARABIC PLATE — 4.0s hold, no VO <<
It is usually rendered: protect yourselves and your families from a Fire.
That verb, qu, is the bare imperative of the same root as taqwa.
Not fear a fire. Guard against one. The command is protective, not emotional.
Notice what that allows: you can be told to guard your family.
You cannot be told to fear on their behalf.

--- 04 · THE FIRE · budget 74 · actual 67 ----------------------------------------
Surah Al-Baqarah, verse twenty-four, does the same thing.
Fattaqu an-nara allati waquduha an-nasu wal-hijarah.
   >> ARABIC PLATE — 3.0s hold, no VO <<
So guard yourselves against the Fire whose fuel is people and stones.
The verb takes the harm as its object. You guard against a thing.
Fear does not work that way in this construction.
One caution: waqud, the fuel, is from a different root — waw, qaf, dal. The similarity is sound, not meaning.

--- 05 · THE GARMENT · budget 82 · actual 73 -------------------------------------
Surah Al-A'raf, verse twenty-six, gives the image directly.
Wa libasu at-taqwa dhalika khayr.
   >> ARABIC PLATE — 4.0s hold, no VO <<
And the garment of taqwa — that is better.
The verse is about clothing. Allah mentions clothing that covers, and adornment.
Then names a third garment, and it is taqwa.
[thoughtful] A garment is not an emotion. It is worn. It sits between you and the weather.
If taqwa meant fear, this line would be strange. As a covering, it is exact.

--- 06 · THE PROVISION · budget 79 · actual 72 -----------------------------------
Surah Al-Baqarah, verse one hundred and ninety-seven.
Wa tazawwadu fa-inna khayra az-zadi at-taqwa.
   >> ARABIC PLATE — 3.0s hold, no VO <<
Take provision — and the best provision is taqwa.
The context is hajj. Travellers, carrying what they will need.
Provision is packed before the journey and spent during it.
[serious] Again the word is placed where a thing goes. Not where a feeling goes.
Nobody packs fear. You pack what protects you.
That is three verses now: worn, carried, held up.

--- 07 · WHERE FEAR LIVES · budget 91 · actual 84 --------------------------------
So where does fear live in the Quran? It has its own vocabulary.
Khawf is the alarm — the plain word for being afraid. La khawfun alayhim wa la hum yahzanun.
   >> ARABIC PLATE — 3.0s hold, no VO <<
No fear upon them, nor will they grieve. That is Baqarah thirty-eight, and it repeats.
Khashyah is different again — a fear that comes with knowledge and weight.
Surah Fatir, verse twenty-eight.
   >> ARABIC PLATE — 3.0s hold, no VO <<
Only those of His servants who have knowledge hold khashyah of Allah.
[thoughtful] Three separate words. Translators collapse two of them into the third.

--- 08 · THE PIVOT · budget 53 · actual 40 ---------------------------------------
Here is the difference in one line.
Khawf is what you feel. Khashyah is what you feel when you understand.
[serious] Taqwa is what you build because of both.
The first two happen to you. The third is something you do.

--- 09 · THE THORN PATH · budget 91 · actual 73 ----------------------------------
There is a report that carries this better than any definition.
Umar ibn al-Khattab asked Ubayy ibn Ka'b what taqwa is.
Ubayy asked him: have you ever walked a path full of thorns?
Umar said yes.
He asked: what did you do?
Umar said: I gathered my clothes, and I was careful.
[calm] Ubayy said: that is taqwa.
Not the fear of the thorns. The gathering of the garment. The care in the step.

--- 10 · THE LEXICON · budget 106 · actual 103 -----------------------------------
The lexicographers say the same thing more plainly.
Al-Raghib al-Isfahani, in his Mufradat, defines taqwa as placing the self in a wiqayah — in protection — from what is feared.
Fear is still in that sentence. It just isn't what the word names.
Fear is what you are protecting yourself from. Taqwa is the protecting.
Scholars differ on its degree, not its shape.
Talq ibn Habib's definition is an action from beginning to end — to act in obedience to Allah, upon light from Allah, hoping for the reward of Allah; and to leave disobedience to Allah, upon light from Allah, fearing the punishment of Allah.

--- 11 · THE MEASURE · budget 74 · actual 63 -------------------------------------
Surah Al-Hujurat, verse thirteen, makes it the measure.
Inna akramakum inda Allahi atqakum.
   >> ARABIC PLATE — 3.0s hold, no VO <<
The most honoured of you before Allah is the one with the most taqwa.
[serious] Atqakum is a comparative. It assumes taqwa comes in amounts.
Fear is not measured that way. A shield is. You can have more of it, or less.
The verse ranks people by what they have built.

--- 12 · THE WAY OUT · budget 84 · actual 73 -------------------------------------
Surah At-Talaq, verses two and three.
Wa man yattaqi Allaha yaj'al lahu makhrajan, wa yarzuqhu min haythu la yahtasib.
   >> ARABIC PLATE — 3.0s hold, no VO <<
Whoever guards himself with Allah, He makes for him a way out — and provides for him from where he does not expect.
The verb is yattaqi. The same root again, in the present tense.
An ongoing act, not a mood that comes and goes.
[calm] The outcome attached to it is a door. Something opens.

--- 13 · THE HARDEST VERSE · budget 65 · actual 64 -------------------------------
One last verse, because it is the hardest one.
Ali Imran, one hundred and two: ittaqu Allaha haqqa tuqatih.
   >> ARABIC PLATE — 3.0s hold, no VO <<
Give Allah the taqwa that is His right.
[thoughtful] The mufassirun differ on it — some read it as abrogated by Taghabun sixteen: guard yourselves as much as you are able.
Others read it as explaining the first, not replacing it. The difference is named, not settled.

--- 14 · THE CLOSE · budget 77 · actual 64 ---------------------------------------
So read the verses again with the right word in place.
Every command to have taqwa is a command to put something between yourself and harm.
[calm] It does not ask how you feel about Allah. It asks what you have built.
That is a question you can answer today, and answer better tomorrow.
[warmly] This channel explains; it issues no ruling. New study every week.

ACCURACY — the claims that have to survive a comment section
================================================================================

1. THE ROOT. Taqwa is from waw-qaf-ya (w-q-y), whose sense is to guard, protect,
   preserve; wiqayah is a protection or covering. This is the standard lexical
   position, not a reading invented for the video.

2. WAQUD IS NOT THE SAME ROOT. The fuel in Al-Baqarah 2:24 is waqud, from
   waw-qaf-dal — to kindle. The film says so out loud at beat 04 rather than
   waiting for the comment. Keep that line even if you cut for time.

3. "FEAR IS STILL IN THE DEFINITION." Al-Raghib's Mufradat defines taqwa as
   placing the self in a wiqayah from what is feared. The video does not claim
   fear is absent — it claims fear is the thing guarded against, not the thing
   the word names. That distinction is the whole video and it is defensible;
   the overclaim ("taqwa has nothing to do with fear at all") is not. Do not
   let the thumbnail's "ISN'T FEAR" pull the script toward the overclaim: the
   thumbnail is the question, beat 10 is the answer.

4. THE THORN PATH. The Umar–Ubayy exchange is an athar — a report from the
   companions widely cited by the mufassirun — not a hadith of the Prophet ﷺ.
   The VO says "there is a report", which is accurate. Do not upgrade it.

5. ALI IMRAN 3:102. Some mufassirun held it abrogated by At-Taghabun 64:16;
   others held 64:16 explains rather than replaces it. Both readings are named
   at beat 13 and neither is adopted. This is the channel's posture and it is
   also the correct one — a verdict here invites the one argument that cannot
   be won in a reply.

6. NO RULING ANYWHERE. The script contains no obligation, no prohibition, no
   "you must". It describes what the Arabic says and what the scholars said.
   Keep it that way through the edit.

7. THE ARABIC. Typeset only, from a verified mushaf, per Track 2. Nothing in
   this file is a substitute for the mushaf — the transliterations here exist to
   identify the clause, not to be set on screen in place of it.
