# Script divergences — batches 11 and 15

These two batches were reworded during generation to get past policy
triggers. Every other batch matches the script pack once transcription
artefacts are discounted.

The changes soften rather than cut. The script pack's own note asks for
the opposite: *"Each sentence is written to survive deletion, so anything
you cannot source can be cut rather than softened."*

This matters for alignment specifically: on-screen captions and the
typeset Arabic are cut from the script text, so wherever the audio says
something else, the caption will not match what is spoken.

---

## Batch 11

**As scripted:**

> Two short surahs from this period show you how directly the revelation was engaged with what was happening on the ground that week.
> Al-Masad. Five verses, and it is about one specific man — Abu Lahab, the Prophet's own uncle, who opposed him publicly and viciously. The surah names him. Not a category, not a type. A man in the family, named, and his wife with him.
> Al-Kawthar. Three verses, the shortest surah in the Quran. The context reported for it is that the Prophet's sons had died in infancy, and an opponent said of him: he is abtar. Cut off. No male line. Finished, and forgotten the moment he dies.
> And the answer is three verses long. Indeed We have given you al-Kawthar. So pray to your Lord and sacrifice. Indeed it is the one who hates you who is cut off.
> [quietly] Fourteen hundred years later, the name of the man who said it survives only as a footnote in the story of the man he said it about.
> That is what early Meccan revelation is. Not a system being handed down in the abstract. A voice answering, in real time, the specific cruelty of a specific Tuesday.

**As recorded:**

> Two short suras from this period show you how directly the revelation was engaged with what was happening on the ground that week. Al Masad, five verses and it is about one specific man, Abu Lahab, the Prophet's own uncle, who publicly rejected his message. The sura names him, not a category, not a type, a man in the family, named alongside his wife. Al Kaothar, three verses, the shortest sura in the Qur'an. The historical context is that the Prophet had lost his sons in infancy and a critic claimed he was Abtar, without succession, no descendants, destined to be forgotten. And the answer is three verses long. Indeed, we have given you Al Kaothar, so pray to your Lord and offer devotion. Indeed, it is the one who stands against you who remains without legacy. Fourteen hundred years later, the name of the man who said it survives only as a footnote in the story of the man he spoke about. That is what early Meccan revelation is, not a system being handed down in the abstract, a voice answering in real time the specific challenges of a specific Tuesday.

## Batch 15

**As scripted:**

> Then it gets worse, and the surahs know it.
> Around six hundred and sixteen, Quraysh impose a boycott on the Prophet's clan, Banu Hashim. Not a fight — a slow squeeze. No trade with them. No marriage into them. No one sells them food. The clan withdraws into a valley enclosure, Shi'b Abi Talib, and stays there, by most accounts, for around three years. The reports describe people eating leaves.
> Then, in roughly the year six hundred and nineteen, two things happen close together.
> [quietly] Khadija dies. She is the first person who believed him, the woman who told him he was not going mad on the night he came down from Hira convinced that he was, and by every account the person he was closest to on earth. Twenty-five years of marriage.
> And Abu Talib dies. His uncle, who never accepted the message and never once handed him over. The reason nobody in Mecca had been able to kill him for ten years was that Abu Talib's protection stood in the way, and now it does not.
> [solemn] The tradition calls that year Am al-Huzn. The Year of Sorrow.
> Within months he walks to Ta'if, sixty miles, to ask another city to hear him. They refuse. He is driven out of it by a crowd throwing stones, and leaves bleeding.
> That is the low point. And it is worth sitting in it, because of what arrives on the other side.

**As recorded:**

> Then the situation becomes even heavier and the surahs reflect it. Around 616, Quraish imposed a strict social barrier on the prophet's clan, Banu Hashim. Not a conflict, a slow distancing. No trade with them, no marriage into them. Everyday commerce is halted. The clan withdraws into a valley enclosure, Sheeb Abi Talib, and stays there, by most accounts, for around three years. The reports describe people facing profound scarcity. Then, in roughly the year 619, two things happen close together. He loses Khadija. She is the first person who believed him, the woman who reassured him he was completely grounded on the night he came down from Hira, questioning himself, and by every account, the person he was closest to on earth. 25 years of marriage, and he loses Abi Talib, his uncle, who never accepted the message, but never once surrendered him. The reason nobody in Mecca had been able to reach him for 10 years was that Abi Talib's presence stood in the way, and now it is gone. The tradition calls that year Am Al Husan, the year of sadness. Within months he walks to Taif, 60 miles, to ask another city to hear him. They refuse. He is turned away by the locals and departs in sheer exhaustion. That is the lowest moment, and it is worth sitting in it because of what arrives on the other side.
