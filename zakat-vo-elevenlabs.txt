WHAT THIS FILE IS

The Zakat script with ElevenLabs v3 audio tags placed for retention, not for
decoration. Section 1 is the block you paste into ElevenLabs. Everything after
it is why the tags sit where they sit, what to do when the model ignores them,
and the clean text for captions.

THE SCRIPT ITSELF IS UNCHANGED. Not one word added, removed or reordered. The
only additions are bracketed tags, three ellipses, and two capitalised words —
all of them removable without touching the writing.

TAG SYNTAX NOTE. v3 only. v2 and Turbo will read "[serious]" aloud as the word
"serious" — if you are on an older model, use the punctuation-only fallback in
Section 5 instead.

2 · THE DELIVERY ARC — what the tags are actually buying
================================================================================

Retention on a 2-minute finance vertical is lost in three specific places. Every
tag above is spent defending one of them.

  0:00–0:08   THE COLD CLAIM.        [quietly] → [serious]
              The hook is an accusation, so it must not sound like an ad. Quiet
              beats loud here: dropping volume on "two and a half percent of
              your savings" makes the viewer lean in, and the ellipsis buys the
              beat of silence that makes "was never yours" land as a verdict.
              This is the single most important line in the file.

  0:08–0:35   THE ENGINE SWAP.       [matter-of-fact] vs [serious], alternating
              The whole argument is a contrast, so the VO alternates registers
              line by line — flat for the bank, weighted for Zakat. The viewer
              hears the opposition before they've finished parsing it. Never let
              both halves of a contrast pair carry the same tag.

  0:35–0:50   THE TURN.              [curious] → [confident] → [thoughtful]
              "Put it to work" is where the video stops being a complaint and
              becomes a system. [curious] opens the door, [confident] closes it.
              [thoughtful] on "it never asks you to give wealth away" is the
              reframe beat — the line most likely to be quoted in comments.

  0:50–1:05   THE ACCUSATION.        [serious] on "most people underpay"
              First direct hit on the viewer. Do not soften it, do not rush it.
              This is the retention save at the one-minute mark, where verticals
              die — the viewer stays because they now suspect they're wrong.

  1:05–1:20   THE INVENTORY.         [quickly] then [serious]
              Lists lose people. [quickly] turns the counts-list into rhythm so
              it plays as momentum rather than admin, then the register drops
              hard on "your home doesn't" so the exemptions register as relief.

  1:20–1:40   THE PROMISE RESET.     [urgent] on "Two minutes. Three steps."
              The strongest anti-drop-off device in the script. "Two minutes"
              is a fresh contract offered at the exact moment attention sags —
              it must sound like an offer, not a heading. Pace up here.

  1:40–1:55   THE DROP.              [emphatic] → [slowly] → [thoughtful] → [quietly]
              Descending energy, rising weight. The callback "was never yours"
              is tagged [serious] with the same ellipsis as the hook so it rhymes
              with the opening by sound, not just by words. Then decelerate all
              the way to the last line — the final clause should be the slowest
              in the video.

3 · GENERATION SETTINGS
================================================================================

MODEL          Eleven v3. Tags are inert on v2 / Turbo / Flash.

STABILITY      Natural. Start here.
               → Creative only if tags are being under-performed; it obeys
                 harder but drifts in timbre between takes, which shows up as
                 tonal jumps between blocks.
               → Robust ignores tags almost entirely. Do not use for this.

SPEED          1.0. The script is already paced by punctuation. Global speed
               changes flatten the [slowly] / [quickly] contrast, which is the
               main thing making this listenable.

VOICE          One with a low, unhurried floor and real range. This script
               needs to whisper and to press. Voices that sit permanently at
               "confident narrator" will flatten the entire arc — every line
               reads as the same line, and the contrast structure dies.

BLOCKS         Do NOT generate all 265 words in one shot. Split at the blank
               lines into 4 blocks, matching Section 2:
                 A  hook + engine swap        (through "pushes it out")
                 B  the turn                  (through "makes stillness expensive")
                 C  accusation + inventory    (through "wealth asleep is charged")
                 D  calculator + drop         (to the end)
               Use request stitching / previous-text so prosody carries across
               the joins. Blocks let you re-roll one bad line without losing a
               good take of everything else.

HOOK TAKES     Generate block A on its own 5–6 times minimum before you touch
               the rest. The first six seconds decide the video's ceiling; every
               other block is replaceable, that one isn't.

4 · TAG-BY-TAG, AND WHAT TO SWAP IF IT MISFIRES
================================================================================

  [quietly]        Volume drop, intimacy. If the voice goes breathy or slurred,
                   swap to [whispers] for the hook only, or drop the tag and
                   rely on the ellipsis.
  [serious]        The workhorse. Weight without volume. Safe everywhere.
  [matter-of-fact] Deliberately flat. This is a tag doing work by NOT emoting —
                   it exists so the [serious] lines beside it read as heavier.
                   If it comes back bored rather than flat, swap to [deadpan].
  [slowly]         Deceleration on the aphorisms. If it drags into parody,
                   delete the tag and keep the [short pause].
  [short pause]    Beat, not a breath. If it renders as dead air, replace with
                   an em-dash.
  [curious]        Slight upward lift. Opens the solution section.
  [confident]      Lands the payoff of a pair. If it tips into salesy, swap to
                   [assured] or remove — the writing already carries it.
  [thoughtful]     Reflective, slightly slower. Carries the two reframe lines.
  [quickly]        Rhythm on the list only. Never use it on a line with a number
                   in it — comprehension drops and this script's numbers matter.
  [urgent]         Used exactly once, on purpose. Its entire power is that no
                   other line in the video sounds like it. Do not add a second.
  [emphatic]       Least standard tag here. If it's ignored, swap to [firm], or
                   delete it — "every other system charges your work" is strong
                   enough unmarked.

  CAPS   Two instances only: "KEEP still". Nothing else is capitalised, so it
         gets a real emphasis lift rather than a shout. If your voice over-reacts
         to caps, lowercase it — the sentence still works.

  "..."  Three instances: the hook, "sits still... shrinks", and the callback.
         All three are the same device — a held beat before a verdict. Keep them
         or remove them together; mixing breaks the rhyme between open and close.

  NOT USED, DELIBERATELY: [laughs], [chuckles], [sarcastic], [sighs], [excited].
  This is a religious-finance argument delivered to a viewer who is about to be
  told they've been underpaying. Any performed amusement or fatigue in the read
  reads as mockery of the subject and torches the comment section. Keep the
  register respectful and certain throughout — the tags escalate weight, never
  attitude.

  TAG DENSITY. Never stack two tags on one phrase, e.g. "[serious][slowly]".
  v3 degrades into artefacts. One tag, one beat. Where this file has two tags
  in a line, they sit on two separate sentences.


================================================================================
5 · FALLBACK — PUNCTUATION-ONLY (v2 / Turbo / any non-tag model)
================================================================================

Same performance shape, achieved with pacing alone. Strip every bracket from
Section 1 and keep the ellipses, em-dashes and line breaks. Then add these:

  · Comma before the second half of every contrast pair — forces the register
    change the tags were buying. "Same pile, opposite engines."
  · Em-dash instead of the full stop in "Two minutes — three steps." to keep
    the calculator moving as one urgent unit.
  · Full stop after "shrinks." held with a line break, so the model breathes.

You will lose the [quietly] hook and the [urgent] reset — those two are worth
enough on their own to justify staying on v3.
