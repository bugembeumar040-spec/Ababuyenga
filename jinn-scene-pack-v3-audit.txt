JINN — SCENE PACK v3 · ENGAGEMENT AUDIT AND REBUILD
30 shots audited · 34 added · 2 rewritten · thumbnail rebuilt · 64 shots total
================================================================================

VERDICT

The 30 prompts are good images. They are not yet a good video. The problem is
not the art direction — it's density, repetition, and a rule that has stopped
earning its keep now that the style has changed. Four findings below, ranked by
how much retention each one costs. Then the rebuild.


================================================================================
FINDING 1 — 22 SECONDS PER IMAGE. THIS IS THE WHOLE PROBLEM.
================================================================================

  671 seconds of runtime ÷ 30 stills = 22.4 seconds on screen per image.

Explainer channels change image every 4–8 seconds. Slow documentary sits around
10–12. Nothing holds a static frame for 22 seconds and keeps a scrolling
audience — the eye finishes reading an image in about three seconds and then has
nothing to do but notice it's bored. A 5% push does not fix that; it's below the
threshold of conscious motion, which is exactly why it was specced that way.

Worst offenders, per beat:

    12 whisperer      64s / 2 shots = 32.0s per image   ← contains the thesis
    11 traveller      59s / 2 shots = 29.5s per image
    05 not all evil   58s / 2 shots = 29.0s per image
    13 close          56s / 2 shots = 28.0s per image
    03 made of        25s / 1 shot  = 25.0s per image
    10 fraud + CTA    23s / 1 shot  = 23.0s per image
    09 death  PEAK    82s / 4 shots = 20.5s per image   ← your best story

Beat 12 is the payoff the entire open was built to reach, and it is two images
across a minute. Beat 09 is the emotional peak — a king dead on his feet while
the work continues around him — and it gets four still lifes.

  FIX. 64 shots, not 30. That lands at 10.5s average, which is the slow end of
  workable and correct for this subject. The added shots are below.

  The six texture plates do not count toward this. They are for overruns, and if
  you use them as filler to hit the number the video will feel padded.

FINDING 2 — TWELVE OF THIRTY SHOTS ARE THE SAME COMPOSITION.
================================================================================

Single object, centred low, long shadow thrown right, open space above:

  S01 table · S06 shield · S09 card · S11 scales · S16 vessels · S18 basin
  S19 chain · S20 staff · S23 fallen staff · S24 table · S26 stone · S29 shield

That's 40% of the film in one framing, and because they share a palette, a light
angle and an ink weight, they will read as *the same picture* with the object
swapped. The consistency rules that make the series feel authored are the same
rules that make it feel repetitive at this density.

Group the rest and the real variety count drops further: 4 mashrabiya shots
(S02, S04, S27, S28), 5 stone interiors (S03, S08, S17, S21, S30), 4 ground and
desert (S10, S12, S13, S25). Thirty prompts, roughly six distinct looks.

  FIX. The added shots deliberately break framing: overheads, POV reversals, a
  technical cutaway drawing, close crops on action, and figures at three
  different scales. Variety of *shot type* is what buys you the runtime, not
  variety of subject.


================================================================================
FINDING 3 — THE NO-FIGURES RULE HAS INVERTED ON YOU.
================================================================================

I recommended keeping the film figure-free two turns ago. That was right for the
photoreal dark pack and it is wrong now. The reason is structural, not aesthetic.

Absence only reads as absence against presence. In the original pack, near-black
ground and hard raking light did the emotional work — an empty room was *ominous*
because the grammar was ominous. On warm parchment in a hand-drawn wash, an empty
room is not ominous. It's decorative. Thirty consecutive empty frames don't
accumulate into dread; they accumulate into a screensaver.

And it costs you the single best shot in the film. S28 — "some of it is sitting
across the table from you" — works because the chair is empty. But if no human
has appeared in nine minutes, that chair isn't empty. It's just furniture, same
as every other frame. You cannot spend an absence you never funded.

  FIX. Faceless figures become standard, not an optional block. The doctrinal
  guardrails do not move an inch:

    · No face is drawn anywhere in the film. Not once, not in any shot.
    · Figures are seen from behind, cropped at the shoulder or wrist, or laid
      as a flat silhouette wash with one contour line.
    · Sulayman is still never depicted. The jinn are still never depicted. The
      Prophet ﷺ is still never depicted. Those three are absolute and the
      figures below are never any of them — they are anonymous believers, a
      traveller, labourers, and the viewer.
    · Arabic is still never generated.

  GLOBAL NEGATIVE CHANGE, applies to the whole pack now:
  REMOVE these three terms → hands, body, silhouette of a person
  KEEP forever            → face, facial features, eyes
  Everything else in the negative stands unchanged.

================================================================================
FINDING 5 — THE THUMBNAIL IS A BET YOU DON'T NEED TO TAKE.
================================================================================

I argued earlier that a quiet warm plate would stand out in a grid of dark horror
thumbnails. That argument is real but it is a contrarian bet, and re-reading it
against how the grid actually works, it's the weaker call:

  · Parchment on parchment is low internal contrast. In a 320px row it goes
    muddy while the dark thumbnails around it stay legible.
  · The staff is a thin vertical object. At thumbnail scale it's a line.
  · No face and no hands means no human anchor, which is the most consistent
    CTR lever there is.

The curiosity gap in "a lone staff in the dark" is genuine. Keep the idea, fix
the execution — the thumbnail is not in the film and does not owe the film its
palette. Rebuilt spec at the bottom.

MOTION — the other half of the density fix
================================================================================

More images buys you most of it. The rest comes from varying the energy, which
the current pack does not do: every move is a 4–12% push or a 20–40px drift, and
uniform slow motion across eleven minutes reads as no motion at all.

  KEEP the slow pushes as the default. They're right for this subject.
  ADD three harder beats and nothing more — S22 at 10%, S24b at 10%, S20b's
    10% pull. Those three are the only places the camera should be felt.
  LINE-REVEAL on four shots maximum, unchanged: S02, S12, S20, S28.
  WASH BLOOM is the new device and it's free in the edit: hold the frame on ink
    line only for a beat, then let the colour spread into it. Use it exactly
    three times — S06b (the cognate row), S13c (the lamps lighting), S29b (the
    row resolved). It's the reference video's actual idiom and it turns three
    static plates into small events.
  CUT ON THE FULL STOP, not on the breath. This script has more full stops than
    the Anger VO by design; every one of them is a free edit point.
