Each pass ran a different lens. Pass 1 found real errors; all fixes are **applied** to the pack unless marked OPEN.

## PASS 1 — Factual, textual, philological

Checked every surah:ayah citation, every root claim, every hadith attribution, every historical and archaeological claim.

### Errors found and fixed

| # | Claim as written | Problem | Fixed to |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ayat al-Kursi "sits **eight lines** above" 2:282 | Wrong by an order of magnitude. 2:255 → 2:282 is **27 verses**. Exactly the checkable slip that owns a comment section. | "twenty-seven verses above it" |

### Verified correct — no change

- **ر ب و** across *riba* / *rabwah* (23:50) / *arbā* (16:92) / *rabat* (22:5) — all four confirmed. The 22:5 rain verse genuinely uses the verb.
- **2:276** *yamḥaqu-llāhu-r-ribā wa **yurbī**-ṣ-ṣadaqāt* — the same root applied to sadaqah. Confirmed; this is the strongest single observation in the script.
- **9:60** — *al-ghārimīn* is genuinely the **sixth** of the eight categories, and the verse does close *farīḍatan mina-llāh*.
- **غ ر م** → *gharāmā* at 25:65, glossed *lāzim* by the mufassirun. Correct, and already hedged.
- **د ي ن** → *madīnūn* 37:53, *madīnīn* 56:86, *yawm ad-dīn* 1:4. All confirmed. Ibn Faris and Lisan al-'Arab do group them; the script's existing hedge ("a shared root is not a claim that two words mean the same thing") is the right level and stays.
- **ṣaghīr/kabīr** in 2:282 against *ṣaghīrah/kabīrah* in 18:49 — the pairing is real in both. The "I'm not going to overclaim this" hedge stays exactly as written.
- Hadith grades: *matl al-ghani zulm* (Bukhari & Muslim) · martyr forgiven all but debt (Muslim) · the two-dinar janazah and Abu Qatada (Bukhari) · the *maghram* du'a and its explanation (Bukhari) · Al-'Abbas named first at Arafat (Muslim, Jabir's hajj hadith). All correctly attributed.
- *dhikr ḥaqq* as the papyri genre term, and the Vienna/Chicago collections. Correct.

### Upgraded

Replaced with the Roman *centesima usura* cap (12%/yr, lowered by Justinian). That is checkable, and it supplies the **fourth verifiable anchor** §1.6 said to add.

**§7** now carries both roots: *dhū 'usrah* (**ع س ر**) and *maysarah* (**ي س ر**) sit in the same clause of 2:280 — and they are the same two roots as Ash-Sharh 94:5. That was sitting there unused; it's a free second root-reveal in the centrepiece.

## PASS 2 — Structure and retention, against the audited beat sheet

### Findings

**1. The turn lands at 62%, not 73%. — OPEN, remedy drafted.**
The *dayn/dīn* reveal is the video's peak and it arrives at 17:40 of 29:30. The source model puts its equivalent at 73%. My section *order* matches the source exactly (turn → record → scope → artifact); the front half is simply too short in proportion.

The clean fix is not to move anything — it's that **the early historicity beat is missing**. The source runs one at 9:20 ("was this ever actually practised?") right before CTA #1. The pack's artifact (§21) does that job, but 12 minutes too late. Inserting ~90 seconds at 10:10 pushes the turn to ~68% *and* fills the gap. Drafted insert:

> **§11b · WAS IT KEPT? (10:10)**
> Which raises the question this study can't dodge. Did any of it hold?
> Partly, and the evidence is mixed in a way I'd rather show you than smooth over. The prohibition on riba was enforced from the start and the classical schools built enormous bodies of law on it — but they built them precisely because merchants kept finding structures at the edge of it, and the volume of juristic argument about what counts as a sale and what counts as a loan is itself a record of people pressing against the line.
> The documentation command fared better, and we'll come to why we know that. What the papyri show is not a society that ignored 2:282. It's one that wrote things down constantly, in ordinary transactions, at scale.
> The verse was not aspirational. It was administrative, and people ran on it.

Note this pushes runtime to ~31:00. If you'd rather stay near 26:00, insert it **and** take the two cuts already flagged in the spec line — that lands ~28:00 with the turn at ~68%.

**2. §22 opened on a proposition, not an image — fixed.** §1.6 flags this as a source-video weakness to beat, and I'd committed the identical fault. Now opens "Start with the eight bowls," which also cashes in image #31.

**3. The audit's own "you" claim was overstated — audit text corrected.** The source doesn't withhold the *word* "you" until 88% (it says "a man comes to your door" at 4:50). It withholds **sustained direct address about the viewer's own life**. The pack now says that, and the script complies: hypothetical-you in §7, addressed-you only from §22.

## PASS 3 — Production, TTS and prompt safety

This pass found the things that break in the tools rather than on the page. All are handled in the two new files.

**1. Transliteration will be destroyed by TTS.** `yamḥaqu-llāhu-r-ribā wa yurbī-ṣ-ṣadaqāt` — ElevenLabs will either skip the diacritics or vocalise them as noise. Every spoken Arabic phrase needs plain-ASCII phonetic respelling. → done in `vo-elevenlabs.md`, with a lookup table.

**2. Verse references will be read as arithmetic.** "2:282" comes out as "two colon two eighty-two." "AH 22" and "643" likewise. All expanded to spoken form in the VO file; the numeric citations stay in the pack for burn-in.

**3. `ﷺ` will be dropped or mispronounced.** Written out in the VO file.

**4. Bracket collision — this one is subtle.** The pack uses `[pause]` and `[beat]` as director's notes. ElevenLabs v3 **consumes bracketed text as audio tags**. `[pause]` roughly works by accident; `[beat]` is not a recognised tag and its behaviour is undefined — it may be voiced. All director's notes are converted to real tags or to em-dash/ellipsis phrasing.

**5. Image models will put script on the papyrus.** Shots 46 and 47 are the highest-risk generations in the sheet, and on this channel a plausible-looking pseudo-Arabic fragment is worse than no shot. `blank surface` is now in the prompt **and** the negative for both, with a composite fallback noted.

**6. Shot 2 will drift modern.** "Immense crowd on a gravel plain" pulls contemporary Hajj imagery — white ihram, modern tents, Jamarat structures. Anachronism negatives added to that row specifically.

**7. Coins.** Shots 11, 13, 27, 40 all say "blank unmarked coins," but models reflexively stamp faces or script on currency. Reinforced per-row.

## Net position

8 factual errors fixed, 1 unsourced passage replaced with a checkable anchor, 1 structural fault fixed, 1 structural fault open with a drafted remedy, 7 production traps handled downstream.

The two claims I'd still call the script's load-bearing risks — both already hedged in-script, both worth keeping hedged: the **د ي ن** root argument, and the **ṣaghīr/kabīr** echo between 2:282 and 18:49. Neither is invented; neither is universally drawn by the mufassirun. The script says so in both places, which is the channel's whole position.
